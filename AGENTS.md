# 23.4° Shifty

23.4° Shifty is an online web game that aims to recreate the great jetski and snowboarding games of the fifth and sixth console generation era.

## GitHub Game Off 2026

This is a fun little side project by solo-dev Kilian Arjona, a CTO in an enterprise software company with 20+ years of experience on IT. It will be submited as part of the GitHub Game Off game jam running during the month of November 2026.

Kilian is in charge of direction, production, vibes, taste, etc. but all code and art (including sfx) will be created by coding agents.

- Theme: **Shift**.
- We are **not trying to win** the Game Off. Do not consider changes to the game or the repo just to "score more points from the judges". Fun is paramount, jam is second.
- **Build it solid.** Cut scope by cutting content (events, locations, features), never by lowering architecture quality (server authority, cheat protection, proper netcode).

### Concept: one place, four seasons

Following the "shift" theme, we have devised a "Shifting Seasons" arch: **the Tilt** is one year in Costa Nevada, one race per season: summer → fall → winter → spring. Two sports for the jam:

| Sport | Inspired by | Where | Seasons |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jetski | Wave Race 64, Wave Race: Blue Storm | The sea | All four |
| Snowboard | 1080° Snowboarding, the more grounded parts from SSX Tricky | The mountain | Fall, winter, spring (no snow in summer) |

- **A circuit is a place, not a season.** Any circuit can come up in any season its sport rides, in any weather and at any time of day (see Conditions). The season does **not** change during a race; the weather can.
- **Tone: grounded**, not over-the-top. Arcadey feel, but not juvenile humor or grinding on a lava pit. No sim either. Happy, fun in-between.
- **Location:** one "place" (region) for the jam: **Costa Nevada** ("snowy coast"), a fictional region where the beach and the ski slopes are close together. It can feel Mediterranean or Californian (there's a Sierra Nevada in Spain and in California). Each circuit is its own part of it.
- **Season transitions:** at the end of a race the camera pulls back over the whole place and time-lapses into the next season (inspired by Forza Horizon). This is how the theme shows.
- Skateboard (EA's skate) and downhill mountain bike (No Fear, Downhill Domination) are out for the jam. Maybe later, like more locations, if the jam version is fun.

### Build order

1. **Make it work**: foundation and netcode first (a box riding a plane, two browsers, a server-authoritative room, client prediction).
2. Jetski (the bay)
3. Snowboard (the mountain)
4. Menus and customization
5. Polish both sports: audio, real jetskis and boards, feel (tricks, landings, particles, camera)
6. Conditions: season, weather and time of day on every circuit
7. More circuits, enough for a Tilt without repeats
8. The full season change: the camera pulls back over Costa Nevada while the season time-lapses
9. Time trial with ghosts

## Gameplay

#### Pay respects to our greats

Wave Race, Blue Storm, 1080° Snowboarding, SSX Tricky, SSX 3, Tony Hawk 1-3, skate, No Fear Downhill Mountain Biking, Downhill Domination... They are all legendary, atemporal games. Our tone leans to the grounded ones.

### Design notes

- **Gamepad-first.** Carving, leaning and trick input need analog sticks. The Gamepad API works in all browsers (phones too, with a pad paired). Keyboard must work, but second-class citizen.
- **Touch screens** (phones and tablets) play sideways only: held upright, a card asks to turn the screen (a phone's browser can't lock it). On-screen controls (`touch/controls.ts`) make the same input as a pad: a stick that comes to wherever the left thumb lands, and a fan of buttons for the right thumb, which slides from one to another without lifting (on a jetski Trick, Roll, Stunt and Brake, the gas always on, or held on a Gas button as a setting; on a board Jump, Trick and Grab). Never tilt steering. The HUD makes room for the thumbs, the race menu has its own button, the browser's back (a swipe in from the edge) opens it instead of leaving the race, and the screen stays awake through a race. Matchmaking mixes every control scheme. The game plays as it comes in the browser: Settings explains Add to Home Screen on an iPhone (full screen there) and shows Chrome's install, and where the browser can (Android, iPad) the first tap goes full screen, held sideways (best effort on Android, untested).
- **Physics per sport.** Grounded means each sport has its own model: water and buoyancy (jetski), edge carving (snow).
- **Tricks never make you faster**, in any sport: races are won on the line and the clock. Tricks score style points (what style earns is decided later, maybe customization), and a bail still costs you.
- **Split times:** at every checkpoint (and the finish) the HUD shows your gap to the first rider through it, or your time if you are first.
- **Every race starts with its intro, no warm-up.** Riders wait lined up on the grid, held still, until every screen has the course loaded. Then the camera flies over the circuit in a few shots (`game/flyover.ts`; the same shots at the same moment on every screen) under a title card (the circuit, the place, the season, laps or the run's length; weather and time of day join it as rows), comes down behind your rider, and the countdown runs. While the grid waits, the opening shot holds with the card and "waiting for the other riders".
- **The race's end:** once you are through the finish, a compact standings tower at the side fills in as the others finish (place, time or gap to the winner, style points; the rest still racing), and leaves the view to the race. On the results, the scoreboard: places, times and gaps, missed buoys, tricks and style, your place up big; in a Tilt also the points each place earned and the Tilt standings, with who moved up or down. Paper panels like the menus, your row in the season's accent.
- **After the finish the race is on television.** Through the line your race is over: your rider rides on in the sim's autopilot (it ignores your stick, scores nothing, and passes through riders still racing), and a moment later a director cuts between broadcast cameras following it (`game/broadcast.ts`, the cameras per circuit in `broadcast-bay.ts` and `broadcast-snow.ts`): it holds each shot a few seconds, cuts to the camera your rider is coming up on, or when the shot is lost (land, a ramp, the snow in the way), never in mid-air, and the camera operator follows a little behind with a long lens zooming to keep the rider about the same size. The same through the results (the rider kept low, under the scoreboard), and for anyone watching a race they joined midway. The speed and the meter go, the race info and the standings tower stay, your ride is heard from the camera, and Y / △ / P cuts to another camera (the hint shows next to the menu button).
- **Wrong way** goes up after half a second of riding against the course, and down once you've ridden the right way for a moment. Time in the air, in a bail or crawling along doesn't count either way.
- **Name tags** only for other people (bots ride untagged, like the CPU riders of the greats): a small paper tag pointing at the head, only within about 30 m, fading out by 40 m, at the screen's edges and behind the ground (a roller, a swell). The handle's number is left off. No tags in the results or under any camera but the chase camera (`hud.tags.hidden`).
- **About the name**: 23.4° is the Earth's axial tilt, the reason seasons exist. A shifty is a snowboard and skate trick (twist the board in the air, bring it back). Always write it with the degree sign `°` (U+00B0), never `º`. Short name for the game is: **Shifty**, like it was **Tricky** for the old SSX 2. The year of four races is **the Tilt**, after the same tilt (`el Tilt` in Spanish and Catalan; never "Grand Prix", which sounds like Formula 1).

### Jetski

- **The bay** (the first circuit): an oval in the bay around a breakwater, past a small island and along the town quay (land is solid, in the sim); buoys to pass on the correct side (red on your right, green on your left), floating ramps, three laps. A race goes grid → intro → countdown → race → results. Through the finish your rider keeps lapping on autopilot (tricks off the ramps included) while the broadcast cuts between cameras round the bay: on the breakwater by the start and in the middle, on the island, up by the lighthouse, on the quay just past each ramp, on a camera boat off each turn, a helicopter high and wide, and a drone.
- **The cape** (the second circuit): a 900 m loop round a rocky headland in a bay of its own, three laps, about as long in time as the bay's but with another rhythm: out of the fishing harbour (the town on its quay, boats moored along it), under a natural arch through the headland's neck (28 m between its legs), into the beach cove (sand, umbrellas, a chiringuito, the lifeguard tower; the start), a long fast sweeper round the point under the old watchtower (torre de guaita), west along the open sea (two ramps, the swell against you), weaving between the sea stacks, and in through the narrow harbour mouth between the mole's green light and a red beacon on a rock. Pines on the clifftops, the snowy sierra behind. The open water off the point takes the full swell; the harbour, the arch and the beach cove are sheltered (the sim lowers the swell round each shelter, a pure function of position the water shader shares: `Shelter` in `crates/sim/src/surface.rs`). A loop of any shape is a polygon of corners rounded off by arcs (`crates/sim/src/circuit.rs`); its outer limit runs as far either side of the centre line, with chevron boards round every turn and markers along the straights. The land is one terrain drawn from the sim's capsules (`render/cape.ts`): cliffs, beach and quay, with one material whose grass, pines and snow follow the season (`COAST_LOOK`: snow down to the shore in winter); the beach fills with umbrellas in summer, a few in spring and fall, none in winter, and the beach bar opens in spring and summer. Broadcast cameras (`broadcast-cape.ts`): on the watchtower, on the mole's head, up the lifeguard tower, on the cliff over the arch's way out, on camera boats off the point and off the stacks, a helicopter and a drone.
- **Buoys are tall inflatable race marks** on a mooring float, red or green, each with an arrow on its white band pointing down to the side you pass it on. They give way: ride into one and it is shoved aside and under, then swings back up and bobs; you're pushed out the side you hit it on and lose some speed (about a fifth, dead centre), and never thrown off. In the sim a buoy stays on its anchor (`crates/sim/src/buoy.rs`); its swing is a look the client draws from where the riders are. The next buoy has a ring on the water and a flashing lamp on top.
- **Chevron boards** (yellow, black chevrons) float along the outside of every turn, angled toward the riders coming in and pointing the way the course turns, so a bend reads from far down the straight. They stand just past the course's outer limit, so nobody rides into them.
- **Air comes from waves and ramps only.** No jump button: pulling back (lean) as you leave a crest pops you higher.
- **One speed meter, passive:** clean buoy passes fill it and raise top speed; a missed buoy or a bail empties it. Missing buoys never disqualifies.
- **Tricks** score style points: hold the trick button in the air and push the stick to spin (360s) and flip (back and front), or the roll button to barrel roll; let go and the ski finishes toward the nearest upright. The stunt button (the board's grab button) holds a stunt the stick picks as it goes down: no hander (centred), handstand (forward), superman (back), nac-nac (left), can-can (right); one a jump. Stunts take time to get into and back out of (the no hander is quick, the handstand needs big air), and a stunt pays more the longer it is held. A rotation and a stunt in one jump is a combo: every part past the first adds half again. Land crooked, or still out of the seat, and you bail. Pad: A or RB tricks, X rolls, B or LB stunts; keyboard: Space, Shift, E.

### Snowboard

- **The mountain run** (the first circuit): one run down the mountain (~2.2 km, about 90 s), from the summit start hut to the village: wide open slopes with sweeping turns, rollers and tables, tree-lined S-turns past the chairlift, a bench ending in a cliff drop, a winding gully, side kickers, a final pitch. Checkpoints across the run (you can't miss them), then the finish. A race goes grid → intro → countdown → race → results. Past the line riders pull up in the finish area, each at its own spot, with a hockey stop in a spray of snow, and finishers standing there hop now and then while the race goes on. The broadcast stays at the finish: a low camera past the arch for every rider coming in, a long lens up the final pitch, an orbit round a rider standing there, a crane and a wide shot of the finish area; a rider still up the run (joined midway) is followed by cameras beside the run ahead of it, and a drone.
- **The glades** (the second run): one run down the mountain (~1.9 km, about 85 s), from the ridge at the top of the second chairlift to the lodge by a small lake: a flat start on a col, a short steep couloir between rock walls to drop in, a wide bowl whose sides rise, with wind lips and a spine down its middle, pine glades (tight turns, pines in the run on both sides of every turn and in the middle of the straights, rollers, rock side hits, a snow-covered log across the run, a rock lip), a winding creek gully with the creek frozen along its wall, a step-down, and a last pitch with two wind lips to the finish. The same finish area as the mountain run; the broadcast adds a camera on the lodge's deck and one low across the frozen lake.
- **Runs are data in `crates/sim/src/mountain.rs`** (a `Run`: centre line pieces, widths, slope bands, a valley table, features, checkpoints, obstacles; the glades in `mountain/glades.rs`), and one generator makes each run's height function: slope bands along Z, a valley that follows the run (wide or close, gentle or steep as rock), noise; the run carved into it (a level bench, banked in turns, dished in a bowl, tables, rollers, natural lips (wind, log, rock), spines, gullies, berms where the slope falls away). The client draws the snow from those same heights, so what you see is what you ride. A test pins the mountain run's snow bit for bit. What stands round a run beyond the sim (how thick the pines grow, the tree line, netting, the lodge, a lake, rock outcrops, a creek, the lift's top station) is in `render/runs.ts`. The mountain's colours (the snow, how much of the ground it covers, the ground where it doesn't, the needles and the snow on them) are uniforms a race's conditions set (`Mountain.setConditions`).
- **Gravity is the engine:** carve (the edge turns sideways speed into speed along the board; fast turns are wide), tuck for speed (stick up), sit back to skid and brake (stick down), skate when slow. The board flies wherever the snow falls away faster than gravity can follow (lips, knuckles, the cliff).
- **Jump button:** hold to crouch and load, let go to pop (bigger the longer you loaded). Letting go at a lip adds to the lip's launch; a few ticks late still pops.
- **Tricks only score points** (no speed meter): the trick button + stick spins (in 180s) and flips, the grab button grabs (the stick picks Indy, nose, tail, melon or method). Let go and the rider finishes toward the nearest way to land, forward or switch. Land along the direction of travel, flat on the slope and not still grabbing, or you fall. In between is **sketchy**: a little crooked, or slammed into the snow, and you lose some speed, wobble for a moment (weaker edge, no loading a jump) and the trick scores half. Trees, rocks and lift towers knock you down.
- **Look:** 1080° Avalanche framing. The camera is close and low, the rider fills about half the screen's height, the view tips down the slope so the snow rushing past fills the screen to about a third from the top, the horizon leans with the slope, and in the air the camera's height lags so the rider drops in the frame.
- **Snow you can read:** the sun ahead of the riders, shade baked into faces that rise toward you and into hollows (so rollers and kickers show even in flat light), blue dye lines on lips, knuckles and the cliff edge, old tracks streaking down the run, and every board carves a fading track of its own. Fog closes in at a few hundred metres.

### Conditions

- **A race is a circuit plus its conditions:** season, weather and time of day. The circuit is only the place (terrain, buoys, scenery). Conditions are picked for each race and carried in the room's plan; the server draws them from a seed, so every screen agrees. In a room the host picks the circuit and its conditions, or leaves them to the draw; single player and matchmaking draw them.
- **In code** (`crates/sim/src/conditions.rs`): `Conditions { season, weather, turns, time }`, 4 bytes on the wire in that order (seasons summer 0, fall 1, winter 2, spring 3; weathers clear 0, cloudy 1, windy 2, fog 3, rain 4, storm 5, snow 6). `time` is local solar time in 10-minute steps from midnight (72 = noon). A plan is the Tilt or a list of races, each field picked or left to the draw (`EventSpec`); the race server resolves it once, when the room is made, with the seed in its tickets, and a pick that doesn't fit (a season the circuit's sport doesn't ride, a weather its season never brings) goes to the draw. `CourseId::ALL` lists the circuits with their sport: a circuit there can be drawn. Every `World` holds its race's conditions; welcomes and snapshots carry them. On screen, `game/conditions.ts` (sun position, weather along the race); the menus' and the lobby's tables (circuits, climates, daylight, named times) are in `@shifty/rider`, kept equal to the sim's by a fixture both test (`packages/rider/test/conditions.txt`; `UPDATE_FIXTURES=1 cargo test -p sim conditions` rewrites it).
- **Seasons:** the jetski rides all four, the snowboard fall, winter and spring (no snow in summer).
- **Weather follows the season's climate:** each season draws from its own weathers, by weight. Summer is mostly clear, with the odd thunderstorm and no rain or snow; fall has the most storms and rain; winter the most snow; snow comes rarely on a fall or spring day.
- **The weather turns along a race** from `weather` to `turns` (the same value is steady weather): on a loop from the first lap to the last, by the race clock against a par pace; on a run from the top to the bottom, by where you are. On a loop it's mostly steady, sometimes building up (cloudy to rain or storm) or clearing; on a run the bottom is often clearer than the top (cloud and wind up high, sun below). It turns only to weathers the season brings.
- **Time of day:** dawn to dusk. No night races for now: they need real lights and make the snow hard to read. A race starts with the sun at least 5° up and ends before it drops below: 05:20-18:30 in summer, 06:30-17:20 at the equinoxes, 07:50-16:00 in winter. The menus name five times: dawn and dusk (the first and last start), noon, and morning and afternoon halfway between.
- **What touches the physics lives in the sim**, deterministic like the rest: swell size and direction, wind, the snow's type (powder, packed, slush) and how deep it covers the ground. Everything else is a look driven by shader values: snow line, foliage, sea colour, sky, sun, rain, falling snow, fog.
  - **The sea** keeps its waves' lengths and directions and scales their heights with the weather against a fair summer day (fog 0.85, cloudy 1.03, snow 1.04, rain 1.06, windy 1.14, storm 1.25) and the season (fall 1.03, winter 1.06), veering up to 0.3 rad in the wind. Each wave has a height at the start and one at the end; the race's clock (`SeaClock`, set from the race whenever it changes) blends them, so the water shader gets both and the race's `turn`.
  - **The wind** (calm in clear and fog; 3 cloudy, 4 snow, 5 rain, 10 windy, 14 m/s storm) pushes every rider alike, 0.02 m/s² per m/s: a little drift across it, about 1% slower into a storm's wind and faster with it. Its heading is drawn per room, near the swell's on the water; on a run it blows half again as hard at the top and half as hard at the bottom.
  - **The snow** (`SnowCover`: depth, and how much is powder, slush and hard snow; the rest packed) sets the board's snow friction and edge grip: powder a little slower, slush much slower and grippier, hard snow quicker and slippery, a thin cover a little scratchy. Fall: 35 cm up top to 20 at the bottom. Winter: powder, 1.5 m to 90 cm. Spring: hard above the slush line and slush below it, the line climbing from about 70% down the run at dawn to 35% at dusk. Snowfall (and a winter storm) lays powder and covers slush; rain wets it into slush.
  - **Fall's rocks** poke through the thin cover: about one every 55-105 m of a run (`cover_rocks`, placed once per run from its seed), off the tables, rollers and kickers, clear of the gates and other obstacles and 5 m in from the edges. They are obstacles like any other (the course JSON lists them with `cover: true`), knock you down like the run's rocks, and are rounded: pressed against one, you slip round it. Bots steer round them.
  - **Dev seas** (`surface` 0, 2, 3) stay as they are; only the sea as designed (1) follows the conditions.
  - **The look reads it** through `sim-wasm`: `updateWeather(x, z)` (a shared buffer, `weatherFields()`: progress, turn, wind, the sea's height, the snow and how hard it snows and rains), `snowAt(points)`, `waveParams()` (each wave's height at the start and the end), the fall rocks in the course JSON; on screen `Frame.weather` and `game/conditions.ts`.
- **The sun's height follows the season** (the 23.4° tilt at work): Costa Nevada sits at about 37°N, like Spain's Sierra Nevada, so the noon sun stands about 76° high in summer, 53° at the equinoxes and 30° in winter.
- **On the mountain**, fall is early season: a thin cover, a white run through brown slopes, rocks poking through that knock you down. Winter is powder, falling snow and a low sun. Spring is hard snow up top and slush below (slow and grippy), with green meadows by the lower run.
- **On the water**, the cold puts snow on the hills down to the shore and the riders in cold-water gear: always in winter, and in cold weather in fall and spring.
- **The weather can change during a race** (the season never does): on the water a storm builds lap by lap (swell, wind, rain); on the mountain the weather and the snow change with altitude (cloud and wind at the top, sun below; in spring, hard snow above and slush below). In the sim it's a function of race time and position, so it stays deterministic.
- **Every circuit is built for every season it can come up in.** Snow cover, foliage, water and light follow the conditions' values (snow settles on upward faces above the snow line), never painted per season. Every combination keeps the snow readable and holds 60 fps, the heaviest one (a storm at dusk) included.

### The Tilt

- A room races a plan once: one event, or **the Tilt**: a year, summer → fall → winter → spring, one race per season. Each race draws a circuit that suits its season (summer is always on the water) and its weather from the season's climate, **every Tilt has both sports** (summer is always on the water, so at least one of the other three is on the mountain), and **no circuit repeats** in a Tilt, so a year takes at least four circuits (until there are, it's summer on the bay, then winter on the mountain). After the last race's results comes the **podium**, then everyone goes back where they came from (the room screen, or mode select; the player moves on when ready), and a room stops offering "rejoin" once its plan is over.
- Points by finishing place: 10, 8, 6, 5, 4, 3, 2, 1. Tricks don't count. Riders who haven't finished when the race ends get last place's points. The last race breaks ties.
- Nobody new joins a Tilt once its first race leaves the grid (reconnecting is fine). A rider who leaves keeps the points already reported; a bot takes the seat.
- Between races the camera lifts off the course while the screen washes into the next season's colours and name, and the next course comes up behind (the simple version of the season change). The same season card covers a race's first course while it loads. Every race waits on its grid until every screen has its course, then runs its intro.
- **The podium:** the top three of the Tilt on a podium on the town's promenade, the bay behind them: the champion in the middle and highest with both fists up, the others clapping and waving, in their jetski kit (it stands on the bay); confetti and banners in the four seasons' colours, a low camera rising slowly to them, the champion's name, and the final standings with points. A card in the whole year's colours covers the swap. Each screen builds it from what it already has (names, looks, points), so it needs nothing more from the race server.

### Time trial

- **You alone against the clock, on our servers:** on the mode select, pick a circuit and a season it rides, and race in a time trial room: one human, no bots, one race, in clear, steady weather (the time of day is drawn: it's only a look; the race server makes the weather clear whatever the ticket says). Leaderboards are per circuit and season. Time trials pay no credits and aren't matches (`match`, `race_result`): they have their own tables.
- **Retry:** the race menu and the results start the same run over in the same room, straight to the countdown (the intro plays only on the first run). In a time trial the results stay up until you retry or leave (Y / △ / P retries, B / ○ / Esc leaves; a room left on its results closes after 15 minutes); leaving goes back to the mode select. The results place your run among the ghosts you raced. The ask rides in every input packet as a count, so it can't be lost.
- **The race server records every run** (`crates/race-server/src/record.rs`): the rider's authoritative state at snapshot rate (30 Hz) from the start signal to the finish, in the ghost format (`crates/protocol/src/ghost.rs`: a version byte, the circuit and season, the time, the split times, the rider's appearance bytes, then frames quantized to the network grid and predicted from the frames before; about 6 bytes a frame, some 35 KB for a three-minute run). A run through the finish goes to the lobby (`POST /api/servers/runs`, retried, with the shared secret); unfinished runs are dropped.
- **The lobby keeps each player's best per circuit and season** with its ghost (`apps/lobby/src/ghosts.ts`; `time_trial_run` keeps every run once, `time_trial_best` the best with its ghost under an id that never changes content). It takes runs only from a live race server with the secret, and only when the ghost's time, frames and splits match the run. The board ranks bests by time, ties to whoever set it first.
- **Ghosts:** by default you race your best and the record; the board's rows pick others (three at most). A ghost is that rider on their ride (`game/ghosts.ts`), played back by race time between its frames through the same models and poses as live riders, in the ghost look (`render/cel/ghost.ts`): the rider's own paint washed toward a tint (magenta your best, gold the record, lime anyone else) and seen through, a rim of the tint on the silhouette and the outline at half strength. No collisions, wake, shadow or reflection, no name tag: a small see-through label over the head ("Your best", "Record", or the name). It fades as the camera comes close, and out once through its finish.
- **Splits:** in a time trial they come at every checkpoint, every lap and the finish, as your gap to the ghost quickest through that point. Through the finish a new personal best or record is called out, and your new best becomes your ghost for the next try.

### Menus

- **Every menu works with a controller, the keyboard, the mouse and touch.** The stick, the d-pad and the arrows move the focus to the nearest item in that direction; the mouse focuses what it hovers, a finger taps. A / ✕ / Enter picks, B / ○ / Esc goes back, Y / △ / P plays, LB RB / L1 R1 / Q E change tabs, Start / Esc (or its button in the corner) opens the race menu. Button glyphs follow the device you last touched (Xbox, PlayStation, Nintendo, keyboard; none for the mouse or touch).
- Screens (Kilian's sketch): **login** (GitHub, or a 6-digit code by email; Costa Nevada flies by behind), **handle** (first time in), **main menu** (Play, tabs home / collection / settings, the social card; home has your rider up close (from the knees up, like a fighting game's menu), a banner, missions, news; the seasons' colours drift behind), **mode select** at the country club (time trial, single player, room, matchmaking; your rider on the terrace, "add party member" (soon)), **rooms** (a 6-letter code to share, ready-up, the host starts), and the race with its menu.
- **Handles** are `Name#1234`: 3-20 letters (any script, accents, the Catalan `l·l`), digits, spaces and `_ - . ·`; the lobby picks the number. Reserved names and a list of rude words (English, Spanish, Catalan) are refused. A new name once a week.
- **Sign-ups are invite-only** until we open them (`SIGNUPS=open`). The login screen opens on the invite code (an invite link, `/invite/XXXX-XXXX`, fills it in and lands on "You're in!", where you make your account with GitHub or email); players who already have an account log in behind "Already invited? Log in". Making an account takes one use of the code. Nothing fails silently: an email or a GitHub without an account, and without an invite, is told so. Make links with `pnpm --filter lobby invite` (`--uses`, `--days`, `--note`, `--count`, `--list`; `--prod` for production's database and site).
- **Single player** is a race against bots on our servers, but it never counts (no times, no progression). Rooms and matchmaking are recorded (`match`, `race_result`).
- **The time trial** is a panel of the mode select (`screens/trial.tsx`): the circuit (from `CIRCUITS`) and a season it rides, your best and rank, the board (the best ten and your row), each row a toggle for its ghost, and Play.
- **Picking a plan:** single player picks the Tilt or one race with its circuit picked or drawn (its conditions drawn); matchmaking is always the Tilt. A room's host picks the Tilt, or one race with its circuit, season, weather and time of day, each with "Draw" first and offering only what fits (seasons the circuit's sport rides, weathers every season still in play brings, the season's named times once a season is picked; `menu/plan.tsx`).

Laid out on a 1920×1080 canvas and scaled to the window (`menu/scale.ts`); a short window (a phone on its side) gets a compact canvas about 640 tall instead (`data-compact`), where layouts tighten and long columns scroll. ### Customization

- **Your rider from presets, no sliders** (Mii Maker, Tony Hawk's create-a-skater): male or female, then a row of presets per part: skin (6), hair (5 per body), hair colour (6), eyes, eye colour, eyebrows, nose, mouth (5 each), beard and moustache (5, male only), height and build (5 each). Jetski (`summer` in the catalog): head, eyes, top, life vest, bottom, feet, jetski. Snowboard (`winter`): head, eyes, top, bottom, hands, feet, board. Your jetski and snowboard kits swap with the sport in a Tilt. The jetski kit also needs cold-water gear for cold conditions (see Conditions); how it's picked and shown isn't designed yet.

A point is a small change (top speed about 0.6%, turns and grip 4%, landing tolerance 8%, rotation speed 6%), so a setup is a style, not an advantage, and the budget is never earned. Set before entering a match; a match keeps the setups it started with (no changes between seasons). `cargo run -p sim --example setup_lab --release` races bots with each setup to keep them fair (racing builds within about ±0.6% of balanced).
- **The catalog, looks, setups and appearance bytes live in `packages/rider`** (`@shifty/rider`, shared by the client and the lobby), with the circuits and conditions tables (see Conditions). Item numbers are stable forever: add items at the end, never reuse a number.
- Riders are modelled in Blender (`art/rider/`; the head comes from MakeHuman's CC0 assets) with painted and baked textures and heroic proportions, exported to `apps/client/public/riders/` and drawn in the cel look. The same riders ride in races (`render/rider.ts` on the jetski, `render/boarder.ts` on the board, poses in `render/rider/ride.ts`). Both bodies are modelled, each with its five hair styles and both starter kits; an item whose model isn't built yet shows the starter's model in its own colours where the slot is required, and nothing elsewhere. The bodies share one builder (`art/rider/figure.py`; their tables in `male.py` and `female.py`), both 1.78 m tall, scaled to the height presets in the game; hair styles are in `art/rider/hairdo.py`. Eye, nose and mouth presets are MakeHuman shape targets (morph targets on the face), each with its own paint over the face (ink lines, the iris, and how its shape moves the shade line); brows and beards are painted overlays in the hair's colour.
- Rides are modelled in Blender too (`art/ride/build.py`): three jetskis (Garbí's runabout, Xaloc's racer, Ponent's cruiser: hull and deck lofted through their stations, footwells, seat, bars, bumper) and three boards (Tramuntana's freestyle twin, Llevant's freeride swallowtail, Xaloc's carver: outline, sidecut and kicks, with bindings), each painted with its brand's livery and wordmark, exported to `apps/client/public/rides/` and drawn in the cel look (`render/ride/`). A model is one mesh and one shader for all its colourways: the mask texture says where the main, second and accent colours go, and its alpha how glossy the paint is; the jetski's bars turn on their own bone. Every jetski puts the feet and hands in the same place and every board its bindings (the boots centred across it), so one pose fits all. A ride whose model isn't built yet shows the sport's starter ride in the item's colours. Bots wear starter clothes on any of the rides. The collection and the pro shop's rides show your ride with your rider (a board on its tail at their side, a jetski parked behind).

## Engineering

### Architecture

```
Browser (Vite + Three.js + TypeScript, Rust sim compiled to WASM)
 ├─ HTTPS / WebSocket ──► Lobby: TypeScript Worker + Durable Objects
 │                        site, login, matchmaking, rooms, Postgres
 │                             │ race servers register here
 │                             │ lobby signs a "join race" token
 │                             ▼
 └─ WebTransport ───────► Race server: native Rust (Docker)
      datagrams: inputs + snapshots (unreliable)
      stream:    start/finish/tricks/chat (reliable)
```

- **Race traffic is WebTransport (UDP) only**. We use the browser so players don't have to download anything, not to reach office/school networks that block UDP. Like console games: if your network blocks UDP, you can't race online. The lobby (menus, matchmaking) still uses HTTPS/WebSocket, which is fine since it isn't real-time racing.

### Netcode

- **Server-authoritative**, Rocket League / Quake model (not peer-to-peer FGC rollback).
- Clients send **inputs only**, numbered per tick. Never positions.
- Server runs the sim at a fixed tick rate and sends snapshots.
- **Client prediction + reconciliation** for your own rider (this is our "rollback where it makes sense").
- **Other riders are predicted too**: everyone is drawn in the predicted present, extrapolated with their last known input and smoothed on correction, so what you see is what you bump into. No interpolation mode for live riders.
- **Ghosts and replays are playback, not netcode:** the server's authoritative snapshot stream of a run (or a whole race) is the recording; the client plays it back by race time. Independent from the live prediction above.
- **Server-side scoring and timing.** Ghosts and leaderboards only come from server-recorded runs, never from client uploads.
- Inputs are validated (clamped, rate-limited, max lag).
- **Protocol designed for UDP**: self-contained or delta snapshots against an acknowledged baseline, redundant inputs, important events with IDs that are resent until acknowledged. Transport sits behind an interface (`send` / `onMessage`).
- Snapshots must fit one datagram (about 1,200 bytes; Chrome reports 1,211 for WebTransport). Use compact binary encoding.
- **Numbers:** sim at 60 Hz, snapshots at 30 Hz. **4 humans max per room**, 8 rider slots so bots can fill the field.
- **Determinism:** native (server) and WASM (browser) sims must be bit-identical. No SIMD math crates, no platform libm (use `sim::math`, backed by `libm`), no `mul_add`, no hash-map iteration in the sim. Authoritative state is snapped onto the network grid every tick, so snapshots are lossless and re-simulation does not drift. CI checks native vs WASM hashes (`pnpm check:determinism`).
- **Rider collisions: yes.** The server resolves them; clients predict them by running the whole world forward (other humans extrapolated by repeating their last input, bots predicted exactly because their AI is deterministic sim code).
- **Bots** are server-side riders whose inputs come from `sim::bot` instead of the network, through the same sim path. Single player later builds on this.
- **Autopilot is sim state:** a rider through the finish (for the rest of the race), one who joined mid-race (until the next race) and everyone on the results ride with `sim::bot` inputs chosen inside `World::step` (`World::on_autopilot`), whatever their player sends. The server and every screen agree without a message, and such riders are predicted exactly on every screen, their own and others'. They only bump each other, never riders still racing.
- **Clock sync:** the server reports how early each client's inputs arrive; clients speed up or slow down up to 6% (hard resync when far off) to keep a 2-tick lead.
- **A race starts when everyone has loaded:** every input packet says which race of the plan its screen has on show (course loaded, season card lifted). A race waits on the grid until everyone the lobby sent (the ticket's `humans`) has arrived and every connected screen is ready, or for 30 s at most (a late screen joins the race wherever it is). People who left don't count; bots never wait. The server then starts the intro (7 s), and the countdown (4 s) and the race follow by the sim's clock, so clients predict them; race phases are sim state, numbered for the wire (grid 0, countdown 1, racing 2, results 3, intro 4). `devkit bots --loading <s>` plays a screen that loads slowly.
- **Netcode lives in `crates/client-core`** (pure Rust: prediction, reconciliation, clock sync, smoothing). The browser uses it through `sim-wasm`; headless bots and tests use it natively. The race server's `Room` is a pure state machine too, so `crates/race-server/tests/netcode.rs` tests the whole loop over a simulated lossy network.

### Race server

- One Rust binary, many rooms (one Tokio task per room). Docker image based on distroless, about 10-20 MB.
- WebTransport with **`web-transport-quinn` + `quinn`** (the stack behind Cloudflare's production MoQ relay).
- Ports: 4433/UDP (WebTransport, all game traffic). 4433/TCP serves only ops HTTP (`/healthz`, `/metrics`, `/cert-hash`), never published in production. Docker needs `-p 4433:4433/udp` explicitly, or `--network host`. The image's `HEALTHCHECK` runs `race-server healthcheck` (GETs `/healthz` inside the container; 503 while draining).
- Config through env vars: `LOBBY_URL`, `RACE_SERVER_SECRET`, `REGION`, `PUBLIC_ADDR`, `LOBBY_PUBLIC_KEY`, plus `BIND` (default `[::]:4433`), `TLS_CERT`/`TLS_KEY` (domain mode), `DRAIN_TIMEOUT_SECS`, `RUST_LOG`.
- Join tokens: `base64url(claims JSON) "." base64url(Ed25519 sig over "shifty-join-v1." + payload)`. Claims carry `aud` = the race server's `PUBLIC_ADDR`, so a token only works on the server it was issued for, the room's plan (`tilt`, or `races`: one spec each, fields left out are drawn) and the `seed` it is drawn with (the same in every ticket for a room, rejoins included), `humans` (how many people the lobby sent, so the first race waits for them), `look` (the rider's appearance bytes, passed on to the room unread) and `setups` (per sport, checked again by the sim), and `trial` (a time trial: one race with its circuit and a season it rides picked). The lobby checks a rider only wears what they own before signing. Every `RiderJoined` event tells the room each rider's look and setups (clients need the setups to predict them), and bots dress alike on every screen. Format lives in `crates/protocol/src/token.rs`; the lobby signs them (`apps/lobby/src/tickets.ts`; a test pins one token both sides must agree on). `scripts/dev-lobby.ts` signs dev quick-race tickets with the same dev key.
- Paths: WebTransport `/race`; ops HTTP `/healthz`, `/metrics`, `/cert-hash`.
- On boot it registers with the lobby and sends heartbeats (region, load).
- Verifies join tokens with the lobby's **public key only**. A race server can check tokens but never create them.
- Heartbeats to the lobby every 5 s (address, region, version, certificate hashes, rooms, humans, draining) and reports every finished race (`POST /api/servers/results`, retried: its course and conditions, its place in the plan and the plan's length, and every rider's result), both with the shared `RACE_SERVER_SECRET`. **Race servers never touch Postgres.**
- Versions must match: the lobby only sends players to race servers of its own version (the Cargo workspace version, one for the whole game), tickets carry it, and a client on another build asks the player to reload instead of racing.
- A room closes a few seconds after its plan ends, and for 15 minutes refuses tickets issued before the end (`iat` in the claims).
- Graceful shutdown on SIGTERM: no new rooms, wait for the current plans to end (`DRAIN_TIMEOUT_SECS`, 900 by default), then exit.
- `/healthz`, logs to stdout, tick-time metrics.
- TLS, two modes:
  - **Domain mode:** real certificate (Let's Encrypt) mounted into the container.
  - **Hash mode:** short-lived self-signed cert generated at boot, hash sent to the lobby, browsers trust it via `serverCertificateHashes`. No domain needed (LAN, friend's PC). ECDSA P-256, valid 13 days (Chrome rejects more than 14), rotated every 6 days without dropping sessions. The next certificate is made an hour before the switch: heartbeats and tickets carry every live hash (the current one first), so tickets signed around the switch still connect.

### Lobby

- `apps/lobby`: a Worker (Hono) with **Better Auth** (GitHub OAuth and email one-time codes, sessions in Postgres, the admin plugin for roles and bans) on **Drizzle + Postgres** (`src/db/schema.ts`; migrations in `drizzle/`, made with `pnpm --filter lobby db:generate`). Our own endpoints only take requests from the game's origin.
- Secrets: `BETTER_AUTH_SECRET`, `LOBBY_SIGNING_KEY` (Ed25519 seed for join tokens), `RACE_SERVER_SECRET`, `GITHUB_CLIENT_ID` / `GITHUB_CLIENT_SECRET`; `BETTER_AUTH_URL` is the public origin. Email: `MAIL_MODE=console` prints codes (dev); production sends them through Cloudflare Email Service (the `EMAIL` binding, from `MAIL_FROM` = `noreply@4shifty.gg`; `4shifty.gg` is onboarded for sending). workerd has no Email Service, so self-hosted lobbies log the codes as errors for now. `DEV_MAILBOX=1` (dev and tests only) exposes the last code per address at `/api/dev/mailbox`. `SIGNUPS=open` lets anyone make an account; otherwise new accounts need an invite code (`src/invites.ts`, the `invite` table).

- TypeScript Worker + Durable Objects. Runs on Cloudflare, or self-hosted with **`workerd`** (Cloudflare's open-source runtime) in Docker. Same code.
- **Durable Object state is temporary only** (rooms, presence, matchmaking queues), all rebuildable. **Postgres is the source of truth** (accounts, results, ghosts). This also makes workerd's experimental on-disk storage low-risk for self-hosters.
- Use WebSocket hibernation when idle so idle rooms cost nothing.

### Engine

Three.js, using **`WebGPURenderer` + TSL** (Three.js Shading Language) from `three/webgpu` and `three/tsl`, never the classic `WebGLRenderer` + hand-written GLSL. Browsers without WebGPU fall back to WebGL2 automatically. The three.js version is pinned (TSL still changes between releases); upgrade on purpose.

- **Water:** physics-relevant swells are a pure function in `crates/sim` (sum of directional waves); the client shader evaluates the same parameters, so the water you see is the water the physics rides. Visual-only detail (FFT chop, foam, spray, wake textures) lives in the client and never touches physics. No paid water kits: their licenses forbid a public repo.
- **Nothing compiles a shader mid-frame.** Shader compiles block the page (Safari's WebGPU, every WebGL2), so a stage's shaders compile ahead with three's `compileAsync`, which doesn't (`Engine.warm`; riders whose art arrives later warm up through `render/warm.ts`). Nothing is drawn meanwhile: the canvas holds its last frame under the boot screen, the loading sign or the season card (a race's course loads behind its card while the netcode keeps running), and the boot screen and the card stay up until what the screen shows is in (its riders, `Stage.loaded`) and frames run without a stall (`Engine.settled`). The page's entry (`main.tsx`) is a small script that puts the loading sign's "Loading…" up in the player's language; the game (`start.tsx`) loads after it. The club's terrace (mode select) loads behind the main menu (`Engine.preload`), so Play shows it at once. Environments keep one `Fog` each: a new one rebuilds every shader drawn in it. A rider's colours and size repaint in place; only a new shape or outfit rebuilds them. A material's numbers are uniforms, not constants, so materials that differ only in them share one shader. Rider textures decode off the main thread (`ImageBitmapLoader`). Check stalls in Safari's engine too (Playwright `webkit`), not only Chromium.
- **60 frames a second on modest machines.** Resolution is automatic unless the player fixes it in Settings (`game/resolution.ts`): fewer pixels when frames come slower than 60 a second, more again once they keep up, and a step that doesn't help (short of CPU, a slow display) is undone. Pixels stay cheap: no Perlin noise per pixel where the noise texture (`render/cel/noise.ts`) or the vertex stage will do, what never changes is baked (the bay's shore distance), and the sky draws last so only uncovered pixels shade it. Draws stay few: static scenery merges per material (`render/merge.ts`), things that repeat or float are instanced, models made of boxes are one vertex-coloured mesh (`render/parts.ts`), and identical cel materials are made once. Riders are drawn only in view, outlined within 45 m, reflected (the body alone) within 30 m and cast shadows inside the sun's shadow box. Measure with `?bench=1`: `window.__shiftyBench` draws the held frame back to back (`bench(n)`: CPU and total ms a frame) with parts turned off (`ablate("noink,noshadow")`), in WebKit too.
- **Audio:** every sound is ours, synthesized with Web Audio (oscillators, filtered noise, envelopes): no samples, no voices. One-shots (the countdown, chimes, splashes, landings, crashes, menu ticks) are recipes rendered once at boot through an `OfflineAudioContext`, the menus' first (`audio/library.ts`). The ride's continuous sounds (the jetski's engine and hull, the board on the snow, the wind) are live voices driven every frame by the predicted riders (`audio/voices.ts`, `audio/race.ts`): yours in full, the three nearest other riders as cheap voices placed from the camera (distance, pan, air absorption, Doppler). The countdown beeps follow the sim's countdown phase, tick by tick, and the wrong-way cue the HUD's steadied warning. One `AudioContext`, made by the first key or click (browsers hold sound until then; a gamepad press doesn't count, so while sound waits and a gamepad is in use a small sign in the bottom margin says to click or press a key, `audio/hint.ts`) and suspended in hidden tabs: an sfx bus and a music bus into a master and a limiter (`audio/index.ts`). Music isn't in the repo: a player fetches tracks at runtime and plugs into the music bus (`audio.onAudioReady`), which ducks under the countdown and the results; with no tracks the game just has no music. Master, effects and music volumes are in Settings, saved in the browser. The menus tick through `menu/nav.ts`.

### Graphics

**The look is cel-shaded**, like an anime or a hand drawing: Guilty Gear Strive and Dragon Ball Z are the bar, XIII the floor. Code in `apps/client/src/render/cel/`.

- Every surface has two tones, lit and shade, split on a hard line. Shade is a cool tone, richer than the colour. Cast shadows fall in the shade tone, and the shade side catches a rim of sky light.
- Ink lines come from a post pass: silhouettes from depth, creases from normals, edges between objects. Each line is a darker version of the colour under it.
- Riders get an outline (an inverted hull). Their normals bend toward simple shapes (an ellipsoid head, capsule limbs) so shade falls in clean shapes, and their poses are held on twos.
- The sky is a painted gradient with cel cumulus. The sea is banded (shelves near the shore, strokes along the swells, cut-out foam) and still rides the sim's swells.

Blender for models. Characters are low-poly; environments, VFX, shadows and lighting aim for PS2/GC fidelity. Modern techniques are welcome, and so is rendering at a lower resolution than we could. Detailed environments with low-poly characters are fine. Every look change is checked side by side against references.

Blender runs headless for asset work: `/Applications/Blender.app/Contents/MacOS/Blender --background --python-expr ...` (glTF export works).

## Workflow

- **Run everything:** `pnpm install`, then `pnpm dev` (Postgres in a container, the lobby, the race server, Vite), then open `http://localhost:5173` and log in: sign-in codes show up in the lobby's log. For GitHub sign-in put a dev OAuth app (callback `http://localhost:5173/api/auth/callback/github`) in `.dev/lobby.env` as `GITHUB_CLIENT_ID=` / `GITHUB_CLIENT_SECRET=`. Postgres keeps running after `pnpm dev` (`container stop shifty-postgres`); set `DATABASE_URL` to use another one. **Quick race without the menus**: `http://localhost:5173/?name=You` in two windows. URL options (all dev-only, see below): `event` (`snow` = the mountain run, `gp` = the Tilt, or a circuit's id or name, as `cape` or `glades`; the bay otherwise), `season` (a name or its number; summer on the water and winter on the snow by default), `weather` and `turns` (names or numbers), `time` (10-minute steps from midnight, `HH:MM`, or `dawn`/`morning`/`noon`/`afternoon`/`dusk`), `seed` (the draw of whatever is left out, 0 by default; the room's name follows these options, since a room keeps the plan it was made with), `room`, `bots` (0-7), `surface` (0 flat, 1 swells as designed, 2 calm, 3 rough), `scale` (render scale), `water` (`basic` = swells only, for slow machines; the default adds GPU chop, ripples, wake and shore foam, and reflections), `render=0` (nothing drawn: netcode and menu tests; works on the menus too), `lookdev=1` (dev: riders on the club terrace for look testing; `&ride=1` puts them on the sport's ride, with `steer`, `lean`, `grab`, `air`, `bail`, and on a jetski `stunt` (1-5) and `reach` (0-1)), `celshot=flyover|club|hero` (dev: cel look test shots; `inkdebug=line|normal|ink|depth` shows the ink pass), `autopilot=1` (dev: ride with the bot's inputs, to look around a course), `podium=1` (dev: the end-of-Tilt podium with sample riders; `season`, `riders`, `me`, `seed`), `bench=1` (dev: the frame-cost bench, see Engine), `sfx` (dev: the sound board: plays every sound, with sliders for the engine, the board and a rider passing by). F1 toggles the debug panel (stats, fake lag/jitter/loss). `SIGNUPS=invite pnpm dev` tries the invite gate (dev is open otherwise).
- **URL switches, test hooks and debug tools are dev-server only.** A production build reads nothing from the URL's query (the router reads only its own routes, like `/play?mode=`). Players set resolution, water and volumes in Settings. Every switch is read through `apps/client/src/dev-params.ts`, behind `import.meta.env.DEV`. Lint forbids reading `location.search` / `searchParams` anywhere else in the client, and `pnpm build` fails if the bundle has a switch read outside `import.meta.env.DEV`, the quick race (`/__dev/`), the e2e hooks (`window.__shifty`), the debug panel or the sound board.
- **Physics tuning:** `cargo run -p sim --example jetski_lab --release [seed]` prints the jetski's draft, top speed, how turns build up and carry on, how long each rotation, stunt and combo takes off a ramp (and the least air each stunt needs), and how often and how high swells launch it and how deep it dives (into, with and across the swell, with and without pulling back). `jetski_lab [seed] [surface] [course]` ends with the sea on a water circuit by conditions (wave heights and steepness at the start and the end, the wind, the air off the crests), and on the cape how much swell reaches each part of it (the harbour, the arch, the beach, the point, the stacks). `cargo run -p sim --example bot_race --release [seed] [course] [season weather [turns] [time]]` runs a bots-only race on any circuit in any conditions and prints times, missed buoys, tricks, points and bails, and which tricks were landed (`BOT_TRACE=<slot>` follows one bot); `bot_race table [seed]` races every circuit in every season its sport rides, in a spread of weathers, and prints the times against its home conditions', the falls and the tricks. `cargo run -p sim --example snow_lab --release [mountain|glades] [seed]` prints each run's profile (pitch, banks, widths), snowboard speeds standing, tucked and braking, the air off every table, lip and drop with and without a pop, a bots-only race down the run (times, falls and where, tricks, points, cost per tick), and the snow by season and weather (top, middle and bottom, a tuck's speed, the bots' times against the home conditions') with fall's rocks. Circuits go by id or name (`oval`, `mountain`...). Conditions keep the bots within about ±10% of their home time (a winter storm on the water is the slowest).
- **Checks:** `cargo test --workspace`, `cargo clippy --workspace --all-targets -- -D warnings`, `cargo deny check`, `pnpm audit`, `pnpm check:determinism`, `pnpm lint`, `pnpm fmt:check`, `pnpm typecheck`, `pnpm --filter lobby test`, `pnpm --filter @shifty/rider test`, `pnpm test:e2e` (menus against the real lobby, and the netcode). CI runs all of them on every PR. The race server image (`.github/workflows/image.yml`) builds on every push to `main`, and on PRs only when packaging changes (Dockerfile, Cargo manifests and lockfile, toolchain, notices config). CI actions are pinned by commit SHA (with the version in a comment).
- **Tooling:** pnpm, TypeScript 7 (the Go-native `tsc`), Vite 8, **oxlint** (type-aware) and **oxfmt** for TS/JSON/MD/CSS. Their configs changed a lot recently: read the current docs before changing `.oxlintrc.json` / `.oxfmtrc.json`. Rust: rustfmt + clippy, toolchain pinned in `rust-toolchain.toml`.
- **WASM:** `pnpm build:wasm` (cargo + `wasm-bindgen` CLI, which must match the `wasm-bindgen` version in `Cargo.lock`). Generated files in `apps/client/src/wasm/` are not committed.
- **Deploy:** the game lives at `https://23.4shifty.gg` (`4shifty.gg` and `www` redirect there). Merging the release-please PR publishes the release, and `.github/workflows/deploy.yml` deploys it: the race server image to GHCR (`ghcr.io/karjona/shifty-race-server:X.Y.Z`), Neon migrations, the Worker (which serves `apps/client/dist`; `wrangler versions upload` then `versions deploy`, so the token never touches the custom domain: change routes or domains by hand with `wrangler triggers deploy`), then the race server through Coolify (moves `:live` to the version, redeploys, and waits until the lobby sees it; the old server finishes its races first). To redeploy or roll back, run Deploy on a release tag. Secrets live in the GitHub `production` environment (tags `v*` only): `CLOUDFLARE_API_TOKEN` (account token, Workers Editor on `shifty-lobby` only), `NEON_DATABASE_URL`, `COOLIFY_TOKEN` (deploy-only); variables `CLOUDFLARE_ACCOUNT_ID`, `COOLIFY_URL`, `COOLIFY_APP_UUID`. Worker secrets and the race server's env vars are set once by hand (`wrangler secret put`, the Coolify app); their values live in `.dev/prod.env` and `.dev/prod-race-server.env` (gitignored). By hand, if ever needed: `pnpm build`, then `pnpm --filter lobby exec wrangler deploy --secrets-file ../../.dev/prod.env`, and `pnpm --filter lobby db:migrate` with `.dev/neon.env` loaded.
- **Migrations must keep working with the previous release's code** (add first, drop a release later): the old Worker keeps serving while a deploy runs.
- **Containers:** Kilian's Mac has no Docker; use Apple's `container` CLI (Docker-compatible): `container build -f crates/race-server/Dockerfile -t shifty-race-server .`. For UDP from the host use `127.0.0.1`, not `::1`.
- **Git:** never push to `main`. Work on a branch per piece of work and open a PR; PRs are squash-merged, so the PR title becomes the commit on `main`.
  - **PR titles are Conventional Commits** (`feat: …`, `fix(netcode): …`), checked in CI by commitlint. Scopes (optional): `sim`, `protocol`, `netcode`, `server`, `client`, `lobby`, `devkit`, `art`, `audio`, `ci`, `deps`. Local commits are checked too, by a lefthook `commit-msg` hook that `pnpm install` sets up.
  - **PR bodies are one short paragraph at most** (what and why). Details go in commits, AGENTS.md or code.
- **Releases:** release-please keeps a release PR open on `main`; merging it tags `vX.Y.Z` and publishes a GitHub release with the release notes (there is no `CHANGELOG.md`; GitHub releases are the changelog). **One version for the whole game** (Cargo workspace, `Cargo.lock`, every `package.json`), 0.x for now: `feat` and breaking changes bump the minor, `fix` and `perf` the patch. It runs as a GitHub App (`RELEASE_APP_CLIENT_ID` variable, `RELEASE_APP_PRIVATE_KEY` secret) so CI runs on its PRs.
- **Dependencies:** Dependabot opens weekly grouped PRs (actions, cargo, npm, Rust toolchain, Docker base images; `three` comes on its own). `cargo deny check` (`deny.toml`) allows only permissive licenses, known sources and no RustSec advisories; `pnpm audit` checks npm packages for advisories (GitHub's dependency graph can't read pnpm's two-document lockfile, so Dependabot alerts miss most npm packages). Shipped builds carry third-party notices rendered by `cargo-about` (`about.toml`, `about.hbs`): the race server image in `/usr/share/doc/shifty/`, the client in `dist/licenses/` (`rust.md` for the WASM sim, `npm.md` from Vite), so `pnpm build` needs `cargo-about` installed.
