# 23.4° Shifty

23.4° Shifty is an online web game that aims to recreate the great jetski and snowboarding games of the fifth and sixth console generation era.

## GitHub Game Off 2026

This is a fun little side project by solo-dev Kilian Arjona, a CTO in an enterprise software company with 20+ years of experience on IT. It will be submited as part of the GitHub Game Off game jam running during the month of November 2026.

Kilian is in charge of direction, production, vibes, taste, etc. but all code and art (including sfx) will be created by coding agents.

- Theme: **Shift**.
- We are **not trying to win** the Game Off. Do not consider changes to the game or the repo just to "score more points from the judges". Fun is paramount, jam is second.
- **Build it solid.** Cut scope by cutting content (events, locations, features), never by lowering architecture quality (server authority, cheat protection, proper netcode).

### Concept: one place, four seasons

Following the "shift" theme, we have devised a "Shifting Seasons" arch: **the Tilt** is one year in Costa Nevada, one race per season: summer → fall → winter → spring. Two sports for the jam:

| Sport | Inspired by | Where | Seasons |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jetski | Wave Race 64, Wave Race: Blue Storm | The sea | All four |
| Snowboard | 1080° Snowboarding, the more grounded parts from SSX Tricky | The mountain | Fall, winter, spring (no snow in summer) |

- **A circuit is a place, not a season.** Any circuit can come up in any season its sport rides, in any weather and at any time of day (see Conditions). The season does **not** change during a race; the weather can.
- **Tone: grounded**, not over-the-top. Arcadey feel, but not juvenile humor or grinding on a lava pit. No sim either. Happy, fun in-between.
- **Location:** one "place" (region) for the jam: **Costa Nevada** ("snowy coast"), a fictional region where the beach and the ski slopes are close together. It can feel Mediterranean or Californian (there's a Sierra Nevada in Spain and in California). Each circuit is its own part of it.
- **Season transitions:** at the end of a race the camera pulls back over the whole place and time-lapses into the next season (inspired by Forza Horizon). This is how the theme shows.
- Skateboard (EA's skate) and downhill mountain bike (No Fear, Downhill Domination) are out for the jam. Maybe later, like more locations, if the jam version is fun.

### Build order

1. **Make it work**: foundation and netcode first (a box riding a plane, two browsers, a server-authoritative room, client prediction).
2. Jetski (the bay)
3. Snowboard (the mountain)
4. Menus and customization
5. Polish both sports: audio, real jetskis and boards, feel (tricks, landings, particles, camera)
6. Conditions: season, weather and time of day on every circuit
7. More circuits, enough for a Tilt without repeats
8. The full season change: the camera pulls back over Costa Nevada while the season time-lapses
9. Time trial with ghosts

## Gameplay

#### Pay respects to our greats

Wave Race, Blue Storm, 1080° Snowboarding, SSX Tricky, SSX 3, Tony Hawk 1-3, skate, No Fear Downhill Mountain Biking, Downhill Domination... They are all legendary, atemporal games. Our tone leans to the grounded ones.

### Design notes

- **Gamepad-first.** Carving, leaning and trick input need analog sticks. The Gamepad API works in all browsers (phones too, with a pad paired). Keyboard must work, but second-class citizen.
- **Touch screens** (phones and tablets) play sideways only: held upright, a card asks to turn the screen (a phone's browser can't lock it). On-screen controls (`touch/controls.ts`) make the same input as a pad: a stick that comes to wherever the left thumb lands, and a fan of buttons for the right thumb, which slides from one to another without lifting (on a jetski Trick, Roll, Stunt and Brake, the gas always on, or held on a Gas button as a setting; on a board Jump, Trick and Grab). Never tilt steering. On a phone the HUD is drawn on the menus' phone canvas (`menu/phone.css`): your place (largest), lap, score and time in one strip in the top left, a split under it for its few seconds, the speed and the jetski's meter by the race menu's button in the top right, the countdown and at most two popups high in the middle; nothing in the thumbs' halves below the top band, nothing under the Dynamic Island or the home indicator, and the map only with room for it (not with Safari's bars up). The scoreboard shows its eight riders in two columns and takes turns with the Tilt standings; the panel after a race keeps its actions in a rail under the right thumb. While the touch controls are up nothing moves the page (`touch-action: none`), so a race never scrolls or brings Safari's bars back. The race menu has its own button, the browser's back (a swipe in from the edge) opens it instead of leaving the race, and the screen stays awake through a race. Matchmaking mixes every control scheme.
- **A phone's browser:** the game plays as it comes in the browser, designed for 874×292 (Safari with tabs open on an iPhone held sideways). On an iPhone's Safari the page runs a swipe past the screen with every layer fixed in place (`touch/device.ts` `installSafariBars`, `boot.css`), so a swipe up anywhere hides Safari's bars and the game gets the whole screen (874×402 on an iPhone 17 Pro); the boot screen and a chip over the grid say so while the bars are up, and Settings explains it next to Add to Home Screen (full screen from the start) and Chrome's install. Never put a non-passive touch or gesture listener on the whole page: Safari then holds every touch for the page's script and the swipe is lost (`touch-action` keeps the pinch out instead). Where the browser can (Android, iPad) the first tap goes full screen, held sideways (best effort on Android, untested). **Typing on a phone:** the field being typed in (its label, what's typed and its form's button) moves to a bar at the top of what the keyboard leaves in sight, from `visualViewport` (`menu/typing.ts`), and sending a form puts the keyboard away. A finger gets no starting focus (it picks what it taps), pickers show their arrows, and in the pro shop a first tap tries an item on and a second buys it.
- **Physics per sport.** Grounded means each sport has its own model: water and buoyancy (jetski), edge carving (snow).
- **Tricks never make you faster**, in any sport: races are won on the line and the clock. Tricks score style points (what style earns is decided later, maybe customization), and a bail still costs you.
- **Split times:** at every checkpoint (and the finish) the HUD shows your gap to the first rider through it, or your time if you are first.
- **Every race starts with its intro, no warm-up.** Riders wait lined up on the grid, held still, until every screen has the course loaded. Then the camera flies over the circuit in a few shots (`game/flyover.ts`; the same shots at the same moment on every screen) under a title card (the circuit, the place, the season, laps or the run's length; weather and time of day join it as rows), comes down behind your rider, and the countdown runs. While the grid waits, the opening shot holds with the card and "waiting for the other riders".
- **The race's end:** once you are through the finish, a compact standings tower at the side fills in as the others finish (place, time or gap to the winner, style points; the rest still racing), and leaves the view to the race. On the results, the scoreboard, titled with the circuit and its conditions: places, times and gaps, missed buoys, tricks and style, your place up big; in a Tilt also the points each place earned and the Tilt standings, with who moved up or down, and what races next (its season, in its colour, and circuit) with the countdown to it. Paper panels like the menus, your row in the season's accent. The last race's results stay up their time (12 s) or until Continue (it shows after 2 s); then **what comes after the race** takes the screen, never the menus by a timer (`screens/wrapup.tsx`): a paper panel at the right over the race still on television (the cameras keep the riders left of it), or over the podium once its ceremony has played and the player pressed Continue (after a 2.5 s beat). Your place big (the Tilt: your standing and points, the top three and you), the race in its conditions, and what it paid as the lobby's ledger has it (place, style, the Tilt bonus; the rows come in one by one and roll into your credits; while the report is on its way they wait, and if it is late a line says so and they still come in), or one quiet line for what pays nothing (single player, a time trial, a private room raced alone). Then what's next, the first focused: Play again (single player: the same pick straight into a new race through the season card; matchmaking: Find another race, back in the queue; a room: Back to the room, where everyone meets again), Change mode, Main menu; Y / △ / P does the first, B / ○ / Esc changes mode (a room: back to it). A time trial's run ends on the same panel (your time, the ghosts you raced, a new best or record called out) with Retry.
- **A win feels like one.** A result worth calling out gets a line over the results and over the panel after them: a race won (in the season's accent), a podium place, the Tilt won or its podium, a time trial's new record or best, your best finish on a circuit (from the lobby's records of your online races); nothing for an ordinary result (a phone's results leave the line to the panel after them). First to third stand on the podium's base (a stripe of the four seasons under the place), the winner crowned with a laurel like the podium's first block (`ui/laurel.ts`). The panel comes in from the side with a little weight. Its sounds are cues mapped to library sounds in one table (`audio/end.ts`): `tally` (each line of what it paid, a step higher each time), `roll` (the credits counting up), `payout` (the credits landing) and `win` (a first place's verdict). Every ending opens with its first action focused, the one row filled with the accent; a screen that opens under a resting mouse keeps that focus until the mouse moves (`menu/nav.ts`).
- **Disqualification:** five misses in a race (buoys passed on the wrong side on the water, gates missed on a run; `race::tuning::MAX_MISSES`) and the rider is out. Its race ends there: it rides on on autopilot like a finisher, is placed behind everyone still racing (among the disqualified, the later out ranks higher), takes last place's Tilt points, and online is paid last place's credits and no style. Bots are held to the same rule. The HUD counts misses against the five by the race info (quiet at none, urgent at three and four), warns at four ("one more and you're out"), and stamps a verdict over the race at five; then the television cameras take over as after a finish, and every result reads DSQ in place of a time. A disqualification is never a best finish, and gets no winner's line. Time trials don't disqualify (`World::disqualify`): there a miss voids the lap. Nothing else disqualifies: no limit on time spent outside the course.
- **Leaving on purpose:** the race menu's Leave asks first when it costs something, and says what: a race you are still in (online: a bot takes your place and it pays you nothing), or a Tilt under way (online: a bot takes your seat for the rest of the year; you keep what the finished races paid, not the Tilt bonus). Stay is focused.
- **When something goes wrong** (the race server turns the ticket down: full, a Tilt under way, over, restarting, a bad ticket, away too long; it can't be reached or the browser can't do WebTransport; the connection drops mid-race; another release) the same paper panel says so in words, with the way back focused (Reload first for another release). While the race server is silent (2.5 s: a "Waiting for the race server…" sign) or gone, the HUD holds its clock, its popups and their sounds; past 10 s silent the race is lost. The transport reports codes (`net/transport.ts`), worded in the player's language. Once the plan is over nothing is ever reported lost: the race runs on locally. An address the game doesn't have gets its own screen with the way home, and a session that ended goes back to the login saying so.
- **The controls** are rows of the buttons the player rides with, in the device's own marks (a pad's: Xbox, PlayStation, Nintendo; the keyboard's keys; the touch screen's own lines), and what each does, for the sport on now (`screens/controls.tsx`). They stand at the right while the grid waits (the title card has the left), at the left through the countdown's numbers, above the speed (not under "Get ready", and not for a finger: the thumbs are on the labelled buttons), never over a season card or the intro, and beside the race menu's panel.
- **After the finish the race is on television.** Through the line your race is over: your rider rides on in the sim's autopilot (it ignores your stick, scores nothing, and passes through riders still racing), and a moment later a director cuts between broadcast cameras following it (`game/broadcast.ts`, the cameras per circuit in `broadcast-bay.ts` and `broadcast-snow.ts`): it holds each shot a few seconds, cuts to the camera your rider is coming up on, or when the shot is lost (land, a ramp, the snow in the way), never in mid-air, and the camera operator follows a little behind with a long lens zooming to keep the rider about the same size. Fixed cameras stand on what is drawn (a parapet, a stand, a boom off the lighthouse's gallery, over a cliff's rim), with a tripod model where one stands; what hides a rider includes people, cliff rims, the gate's towers and ramps; the drone keeps out of land. The same through the results (the rider kept low, under the scoreboard), and for anyone watching a race they joined midway. The speed, the meter and the map go, the race info and the standings tower stay, your ride is heard from the camera, and Y / △ / P cuts to another camera (the hint shows next to the menu button).
- **Wrong way** goes up after half a second of riding against the course, and down once you've ridden the right way for a moment. Time in the air, in a bail or crawling along doesn't count either way.
- **Name tags** only for other people (bots ride untagged, like the CPU riders of the greats): a small paper tag pointing at the head, only within about 30 m, fading out by 40 m, at the screen's edges and behind the ground (a roller, a swell). The handle's number is left off. No tags in the results or under any camera but the chase camera (`hud.tags.hidden`). CPU riders have names like the riders of the greats, an initial and a surname (N. Ferrer, S. Tanaka…), dealt per seat from the room's id: never two alike, the same on every screen and for the whole plan. They wear every colourway of the built kits, from the room's seed and their seat.
- **About the name**: 23.4° is the Earth's axial tilt, the reason seasons exist. A shifty is a snowboard and skate trick (twist the board in the air, bring it back). Always write it with the degree sign `°` (U+00B0), never `º`. Short name for the game is: **Shifty**, like it was **Tricky** for the old SSX 2. The year of four races is **the Tilt**, after the same tilt (`el Tilt` in Spanish and Catalan; never "Grand Prix", which sounds like Formula 1).

### Jetski

- **The bay** (the first circuit): an oval in the bay around a breakwater, past a small island and along the town quay (the market hall with the town clock on its front, the country club at its east end; a little train runs the plain behind the town; land is solid, in the sim); buoys to pass on the correct side (red on your right, green on your left), floating ramps, three laps. A race goes grid → intro → countdown → race → results. Through the finish your rider keeps lapping on autopilot (tricks off the ramps included) while the broadcast cuts between cameras round the bay: on the breakwater by the start and in the middle, on the island, up by the lighthouse, on the quay just past each ramp, on a camera boat off each turn, a helicopter high and wide, and a drone.
- **The cape** (the second circuit): a 900 m loop round a rocky headland in a bay of its own, three laps, about as long in time as the bay's but with another rhythm: out of the fishing harbour (the town on its quay, boats moored along it, the fish market with its ice house and crane by the mole's root), under a natural arch through the headland's neck (28 m between its legs, 22 m under its crown), into the beach cove (sand, umbrellas, a chiringuito, the lifeguard tower; the start), a long fast sweeper round the point under the old watchtower (torre de guaita), west along the open sea (two ramps, the swell against you), weaving between the sea stacks, and in through the narrow harbour mouth between the mole's green light and a red beacon on a rock. Pines on the clifftops, the snowy sierra behind. The open water off the point takes the full swell; the harbour, the arch and the beach cove are sheltered (the sim lowers the swell round each shelter, a pure function of position the water shader shares: `Shelter` in `crates/sim/src/surface.rs`). A loop of any shape is a polygon of corners rounded off by arcs (`crates/sim/src/circuit.rs`); its outer limit runs as far either side of the centre line, with chevron boards round every turn and markers along the straights. The land is one terrain drawn from the sim's capsules (`render/cape.ts`): cliffs, beach and quay, with one material whose grass and pines follow the season (`COAST_LOOK`) and its snow the conditions' snow line (down to the shore in winter); the beach fills with umbrellas in summer, a few in spring and fall, none in winter, and the beach bar opens in spring and summer. Broadcast cameras (`broadcast-cape.ts`): on the watchtower, on the mole's head, up the lifeguard tower, on the cliff over the arch's way out, on camera boats off the point and off the stacks, a helicopter and a drone.
- **Moored boats are solid:** every boat moored along a quay, in a harbour or at anchor is course data (`Course.boats` in `crates/sim/src/course.rs`: place, heading, length, beam), stops a hull like land, and is drawn by the client's `Fleet` from the course JSON, so what you see is what you bump into; it rocks on the swell as a look. Boats keep 8 m clear of the centre line, of every buoy's passing side and of the ramps (a test holds it). Camera boats and boats under way are scenery outside the course.
- **The start gate** floats: two banded masts on rafts carrying a truss beam, with the chequered banner hanging from it as cloth that ripples in the wind and shades its folds (`render/course.ts`, `gateTowers`; `bannerMaterial`). **Ramps** are yellow with arrows up the deck, red and white skirts and orange floats, in every season.
- **Sea caves and notches** at the water line, fissures and guano caps are a look cut into the cliffs and stacks (`render/coast/rock.ts`), never into the sim's shore.
- **Buoys are tall inflatable race marks** on a mooring float, red or green, each with an arrow on its white band pointing down to the side you pass it on. They give way: ride into one and it is shoved aside and under, then swings back up and bobs; you're pushed out the side you hit it on and lose some speed (about a fifth, dead centre), and never thrown off. In the sim a buoy stays on its anchor (`crates/sim/src/buoy.rs`); its swing is a look the client draws from where the riders are. The next buoy has a ring on the water and a flashing lamp on top.
- **Chevron boards** (yellow, black chevrons) float along the outside of every turn, angled toward the riders coming in and pointing the way the course turns, so a bend reads from far down the straight. They stand just past the course's outer limit, so nobody rides into them.
- **Air comes from waves and ramps only.** No jump button: pulling back (lean) as you leave a crest pops you higher.
- **One speed meter, passive:** clean buoy passes fill it and raise top speed; a missed buoy or a bail empties it.
- **Five misses and you're out** (Wave Race 64's rule; see Disqualification under Design notes): a buoy passed on its wrong side is a miss.
- **Tricks** score style points: hold the trick button in the air and push the stick to spin (360s) and flip (back and front), or the roll button to barrel roll; let go and the ski finishes toward the nearest upright. The stunt button (the board's grab button) holds a stunt the stick picks as it goes down: no hander (centred), handstand (forward), superman (back), nac-nac (left), can-can (right); one a jump. Stunts take time to get into and back out of (the no hander is quick, the handstand needs big air), and a stunt pays more the longer it is held. A rotation and a stunt in one jump is a combo: every part past the first adds half again. Land crooked, or still out of the seat, and you bail. Pad: A or RB tricks, X rolls, B or LB stunts; keyboard: Space, Shift, E.

### Snowboard

- **The mountain run** (the first circuit): one run down the mountain (~2.2 km, about 90 s), from the summit start hut to the village: open slopes fenced in with netting, with sweeping turns, rollers and tables, tree-lined S-turns past the chairlift, a bench ending in a cliff (a rock band about 8 m tall: you leave its lip in the air), a winding gully between rock walls, side kickers, a final pitch. Gates to pass all the way down, checkpoints across the run for the split times (you can't miss those), then the finish. A race goes grid → intro → countdown → race → results. Past the line riders pull up in the finish area, each at its own spot, with a hockey stop in a spray of snow, and finishers standing there hop now and then while the race goes on. The broadcast stays at the finish: a low camera past the arch for every rider coming in, a long lens up the final pitch, an orbit round a rider standing there, a crane and a wide shot of the finish area; a rider still up the run (joined midway) is followed by cameras beside the run ahead of it, and a drone.
- **The glades** (the second run): one run down the mountain (~1.9 km, about 85 s), from the ridge at the top of the second chairlift to the lodge by a small lake: a flat start on a col, a short steep couloir between rock walls to drop in, a bowl whose banks rise, with wind lips and a spine down its middle, pine glades (tight turns, pines in the run on both sides of every turn and in the middle of the straights, rollers, rock side hits, a snow-covered log across the run, a rock lip), a winding creek gully between rock walls with the creek frozen along one, a step-down, and a last pitch with two wind lips to the finish. The same finish area as the mountain run; the broadcast adds a camera on the lodge's deck and one low across the frozen lake.
- **Runs are narrow**, after 1080° Avalanche: about 13 m between what bounds them, about 22 m at their widest (the bowl, the open slopes), 10 m in the couloir and the gullies, and they breathe (tight, open, tight). Each side is a bank, trees, netting or rock (`Run::edges`, `Edge`), never an invisible wall, and riders hit it as it looks (`Run::hold_in`): a bank throws you back, netting catches and slows you and never knocks you down, trees and rock knock you down when hit hard, and rock scrapes.
- **Gates** (`Run::gates`, `GateDef`: two poles 7-8 m apart, 17-18 a run): pass between them. They are laid as a rhythm of turns (off-centre one way then the other where the run is wide, on the fast line where it is tight), never on a takeoff, a landing or the line through the air. A missed gate counts toward the five misses that disqualify (see Disqualification). In the sim they are met in course order among the checkpoints (`Run::marks`) and raised like buoys (`Gate::Buoy`, `EventKind::Buoy`), so the HUD's count, the map, a time trial's splits and voided laps all read them. On the snow (`render/snow-gates.ts`, `SnowGates`): either side of the gap a slalom panel (two poles and a cloth, red then blue down the mountain) that folds over when a rider brushes it and whips back up (a look: in the sim a gate is only its line), a dye line across the gap, and a marker floating over the gate to pass next, with a smaller, paler one over the gate after it. Checkpoints have no flags of their own.
- **Cliffs and rock walls are the sim's** (`Run::cliffs`, `Cliff`; walls where an edge is rock) and go out in the course JSON; the client builds them in `render/mountain-walls.ts`: walls as buttresses along the edge (`rockWalls`), a cliff as a band of rock columns across the run and out into the slope as far as the ground drops, each in two tiers with a ledge, snow on its brow and ledges and icicles under the brows that overhang (`cliffFaces`). Netting stops at a cliff.
- **Runs are data in `crates/sim/src/mountain.rs`** (a `Run`: centre line pieces, widths, edges, slope bands, a valley table, features, cliffs, gates, checkpoints, obstacles; the glades in `mountain/glades.rs`), and one generator makes each run's height function: slope bands along Z, a valley that follows the run (wide or close, gentle or steep as rock), noise; the run carved into it (a level bench, banked in turns, dished in a bowl, tables, rollers, natural lips (wind, log, rock), spines, gullies, berms where the slope falls away). The client draws the snow from those same heights, so what you see is what you ride. A test pins the mountain run's snow bit for bit. What stands round a run beyond the sim (how thick the forest grows, the tree line, rock outcrops, the lodge, a village or not, a lake, a creek, the lift's top station) is in `render/runs.ts`, and is drawn by `render/mountain-forest.ts` (the forest, and the shade it throws on the run, baked per vertex for the race's sun), `mountain-rocks.ts` (rocks, rock walls, the strata all rock shares), `mountain-built.ts` (the village, the lift and its chairs, gantries, flags, sponsor boards, the crowd), `mountain-life.ts` (blown snow, snow off the boughs, smoke, choughs) `mountain-far.ts` (the land beyond the sim's, down to the coast and the sea), `mountain-ranges.ts` (the sierra round the valley: crest lines drawn by hand, per run shape) and `mountain-walls.ts` (rock walls along a run as one face of buttresses, with icicles in the cold; `walls` in `runs.ts`). Lift stations stand where `liftEnds` finds room clear of the riding width, and the forest is cut back under the line and round the grid. What stands tall at the finish (grandstands, the timing board, snowcats) stands up the run from the line, so it is never between the finish cameras and the finishers. Firs carry snow on their boughs by the snow line and sway with the wind; pines grow only on the lowest part of a run. The mountain's look in its conditions is the shared one (see Conditions): the snow on the run is the sim's, off it the snow lies down to the season's snow line, and the run's props stand out in the weather.
- **The run's map** (`ui/minimap.ts`, `RunMap`) spreads the run sideways (up to three times) so its turns read in a tall, thin map.
- **Gravity is the engine:** carve (the edge turns sideways speed into speed along the board; fast turns are wide), tuck for speed (stick up), sit back to skid and brake (stick down), skate when slow. The board flies wherever the snow falls away faster than gravity can follow (lips, knuckles, the cliff).
- **Jump button:** hold to crouch and load, let go to pop (bigger the longer you loaded). Letting go at a lip adds to the lip's launch; a few ticks late still pops.
- **Tricks only score points** (no speed meter): the trick button + stick spins (in 180s) and flips, the grab button grabs (the stick picks Indy, nose, tail, melon or method). Let go and the rider finishes toward the nearest way to land, forward or switch. Land along the direction of travel, flat on the slope and not still grabbing, or you fall. In between is **sketchy**: a little crooked, or slammed into the snow, and you lose some speed, wobble for a moment (weaker edge, no loading a jump) and the trick scores half. Trees, rocks and lift towers knock you down.
- **Look:** 1080° Avalanche framing. The camera is close and low, the rider fills about half the screen's height, the view tips down the slope so the snow rushing past fills the screen to about a third from the top, the horizon leans with the slope, and in the air the camera's height lags so the rider drops in the frame.
- **Snow you can read:** the sun ahead of the riders, shade baked into faces that rise toward you and into hollows (so rollers and kickers show even in flat light), blue dye lines on lips, knuckles and the cliff edge, old tracks streaking down the run, and every board carves a fading track of its own. A clear day sees about 4 km down the valley (`CLEAR_FAR` in `render/mountain.ts`); fog, rain, storm and snow close it in to a few hundred metres, and the mountain's ink lines fade into the fog with what they outline (`fadingInk` in `mountain-look.ts`). Every shade tone on the mountain goes through `shadeKept()`, so shade stays darker than light whatever the weather's light; under a closed sky the groomed run stays lighter than the snow beside it, and lamps glow as the light goes.

### Conditions

- **A race is a circuit plus its conditions:** season, weather and time of day. The circuit is only the place (terrain, buoys, scenery). Conditions are picked for each race and carried in the room's plan; the server draws them from a seed, so every screen agrees. In a room the host picks the circuit and its conditions, or leaves them to the draw; single player and matchmaking draw them.
- **In code** (`crates/sim/src/conditions.rs`): `Conditions { season, weather, turns, time }`, 4 bytes on the wire in that order (seasons summer 0, fall 1, winter 2, spring 3; weathers clear 0, cloudy 1, windy 2, fog 3, rain 4, storm 5, snow 6). `time` is local solar time in 10-minute steps from midnight (72 = noon). A plan is the Tilt or a list of races, each field picked or left to the draw (`EventSpec`); the race server resolves it once, when the room is made, with the seed in its tickets, and a pick that doesn't fit (a season the circuit's sport doesn't ride, a weather its season never brings) goes to the draw. `CourseId::ALL` lists the circuits with their sport: a circuit there can be drawn. Every `World` holds its race's conditions; welcomes and snapshots carry them. On screen, `game/conditions.ts` (sun position, weather along the race); the menus' and the lobby's tables (circuits, climates, daylight, named times) are in `@shifty/rider`, kept equal to the sim's by a fixture both test (`packages/rider/test/conditions.txt`; `UPDATE_FIXTURES=1 cargo test -p sim conditions` rewrites it).
- **Seasons:** the jetski rides all four, the snowboard fall, winter and spring (no snow in summer).
- **Weather follows the season's climate:** each season draws from its own weathers, by weight. Summer is mostly clear, with the odd thunderstorm and no rain or snow; fall has the most storms and rain; winter the most snow; snow comes rarely on a fall or spring day.
- **The weather turns along a race** from `weather` to `turns` (the same value is steady weather): on a loop from the first lap to the last, by the race clock against a par pace; on a run from the top to the bottom, by where you are. On a loop it's mostly steady, sometimes building up (cloudy to rain or storm) or clearing; on a run the bottom is often clearer than the top (cloud and wind up high, sun below). It turns only to weathers the season brings.
- **Time of day:** dawn to dusk. No night races for now: they need real lights and make the snow hard to read. A race starts with the sun at least 5° up and ends before it drops below: 05:20-18:30 in summer, 06:30-17:20 at the equinoxes, 07:50-16:00 in winter. The menus name five times: dawn and dusk (the first and last start), noon, and morning and afternoon halfway between.
- **What touches the physics lives in the sim**, deterministic like the rest: swell size and direction, wind, the snow's type (powder, packed, slush) and how deep it covers the ground. Everything else is a look driven by shader values: snow line, foliage, sea colour, sky, sun, rain, falling snow, fog.
  - **The sea** keeps its waves' lengths and directions and scales their heights with the weather against a fair summer day (fog 0.85, cloudy 1.03, snow 1.04, rain 1.06, windy 1.14, storm 1.25) and the season (fall 1.03, winter 1.06), veering up to 0.3 rad in the wind. Each wave has a height at the start and one at the end; the race's clock (`SeaClock`, set from the race whenever it changes) blends them, so the water shader gets both and the race's `turn`.
  - **The wind** (calm in clear and fog; 3 cloudy, 4 snow, 5 rain, 10 windy, 14 m/s storm) pushes every rider alike, 0.02 m/s² per m/s: a little drift across it, about 1% slower into a storm's wind and faster with it. Its heading is drawn per room, near the swell's on the water; on a run it blows half again as hard at the top and half as hard at the bottom.
  - **The snow** (`SnowCover`: depth, and how much is powder, slush and hard snow; the rest packed) sets the board's snow friction and edge grip: powder a little slower, slush much slower and grippier, hard snow quicker and slippery, a thin cover a little scratchy. Fall: 35 cm up top to 20 at the bottom. Winter: powder, 1.5 m to 90 cm. Spring: hard above the slush line and slush below it, the line climbing from about 70% down the run at dawn to 35% at dusk. Snowfall (and a winter storm) lays powder and covers slush; rain wets it into slush.
  - **Fall's rocks** poke through the thin cover: about one every 45-90 m of a run (`cover_rocks`, placed once per run from its seed), off the tables, rollers and kickers, clear of the gates, the landings and other obstacles and 2 m in from the riding limit. They are obstacles like any other (the course JSON lists them with `cover: true`), knock you down like the run's rocks, and are rounded: pressed against one, you slip round it. Bots steer round them.
  - **Dev seas** (`surface` 0, 2, 3) stay as they are; only the sea as designed (1) follows the conditions.
  - **The look reads it** through `sim-wasm`: `updateWeather(x, z)` (a shared buffer, `weatherFields()`: progress, turn, wind, the sea's height, the snow and how hard it snows and rains), `snowAt(points)`, `waveParams()` (each wave's height at the start and the end), the fall rocks in the course JSON; on screen `Frame.weather` and `game/conditions.ts`.
- **The sun's height follows the season** (the 23.4° tilt at work): Costa Nevada sits at about 37°N, like Spain's Sierra Nevada, so the noon sun stands about 76° high in summer, 53° at the equinoxes and 30° in winter.
- **On the mountain**, fall is early season: a thin cover, a white run through brown slopes, rocks poking through that knock you down. Winter is powder, falling snow and a low sun. Spring is hard snow up top and slush below (slow and grippy), with green meadows by the lower run.
- **On the water**, the cold puts snow on the hills down to the shore and the riders in cold-water gear: always in winter, and when it snows in fall or spring (`coldWater` in `game/conditions.ts`).
- **The weather can change during a race** (the season never does): on the water a storm builds lap by lap (swell, wind, rain); on the mountain the weather and the snow change with altitude (cloud and wind at the top, sun below; in spring, hard snow above and slush below). In the sim it's a function of race time and position, so it stays deterministic.
- **Every circuit is built for every season it can come up in.** Snow cover, foliage, water and light follow the conditions' values (snow settles on upward faces above the snow line), never painted per season. Every combination keeps the snow readable and holds 60 fps, the heaviest one (a storm at dusk) included.
- **The look of conditions is uniforms** (`render/cel/look.ts`, `LOOK`): the light's colour on the lit and the shade side, how much cast shadows show, the sky, the cloud deck and the cumulus, the haze over distant scenery, the snow line, wet, the season's greens, the sea's colours, whitecaps and rain rings. A circuit's environment works them out from its conditions (`render/look.ts`: `ConditionsLook`, with the circuit's `CircuitLook`) and sets them every frame as the weather turns; every material reads them, so neither a season nor the weather ever builds a shader. Scenery opts in on its material: `outdoor` (a `FOLIAGE` kind: none, grass, leaf, evergreen) takes the season's greens (fall's rust and gold, winter's bare trees and dark pines, spring's fresh green and blossom) and snow on upward faces above the snow line, `haze` fades it into the distance (more on a hazy day); standard materials say it through `userData.outdoor` and `HAZE` before `celify`. New circuits get the look by using these.
- **The sun stands where the season and the clock put it** (`sunAt`), never lower than 10° for light and shadows. Each circuit has a compass: noon's sun stands in the direction its light was picked for (on the bay over the town's side; on the mountain down the run), and the mountain keeps about half the sun's swing, so it stays ahead of the riders from dawn to dusk. Low sun warms the lit side and the horizon round the sun and cools the shade; a closed sky takes the warmth away.
- **Weathers:** clear (a clean gradient, cumulus at the horizon, a few faint tufts of cirrus); cloudy (more and greyer cumulus, a broken deck, flatter light, softer shadows); windy (streaked cirrus, flat cumulus, whitecaps and a rougher chop); fog (the fog closes in to a couple of hundred metres, the distance gone); rain (a grey deck, rain streaks, a darker sea with rings on it, wet ground); storm (a dark deck with lit flanks and a shelf cloud on the windward side with shafts of rain, heavy slanting rain, a grey-green sea full of whitecaps, no shadows; no lightning); snow (a pale deck, falling snow, low contrast). In windy weather and storms gusts cross the sea as darker patches and foam is drawn out in streaks along the wind (`cel/water.ts`). Rain and snow fall on the GPU in a box round the camera (`render/cel/precip.ts`; only what is falling is drawn, and under a 50° field of view the box moves out along the view toward what the camera looks at, `aimPrecipitation`, so television cameras show the weather at the rider's distance); on the mountain rain falls as snow in winter and above the snow line, and winter always brings a few flakes.
- **Seasons on the water:** summer turquoise water and a little gold in the grass, snow only on the highest peaks; fall a deeper sea, rust and gold trees and ochre fields; winter a steel-blue sea and snow on everything down to the shore; spring fresh green and blossom.
- **Seasons on the mountain:** the run carries the sim's snow (`Terrain.snow`, row by row): a thin fall cover is scratchy, specked with grass and bare at the run's edges, with dark rocks poking through on bare ground; winter powder fills the old tracks in; spring's hard snow up top is polished and its slush below grey and wet. Off the run the snow lies down to the season's snow line (fall only up high, so a white run through brown slopes; spring above the middle, green meadows below), deeper in hollows, never on steep rock; pines and roofs carry snow as deep as it lies at their height.

### The Tilt

- A room races a plan once: one event, or **the Tilt**: a year, summer → fall → winter → spring, one race per season. Each race draws a circuit that suits its season (summer is always on the water) and its weather from the season's climate, **every Tilt has both sports** (summer is always on the water, so at least one of the other three is on the mountain), and **no circuit repeats** in a Tilt, so a year takes at least four circuits (the bay, the cape, the mountain run and the glades; with fewer, a Tilt is summer on the water, then winter on the mountain). After the last race's results comes the **podium**, then everyone goes back where they came from (the room screen, or mode select; the player moves on when ready), and a room stops offering "rejoin" once its plan is over.
- Points by finishing place: 10, 8, 6, 5, 4, 3, 2, 1. Tricks don't count. Riders who haven't finished when the race ends get last place's points. The last race breaks ties.
- Nobody new joins a Tilt once its first race leaves the grid (reconnecting is fine). A rider who leaves keeps the points already reported; a bot takes the seat.
- Between races the camera lifts off the course while the screen washes into the next season's colours and name, and the next course comes up behind (the simple version of the season change). The same season card covers a race's first course while it loads. Every race waits on its grid until every screen has its course, then runs its intro.
- **The podium:** the top three of the Tilt on a podium on the town's promenade, the bay behind them: the champion in the middle and highest with both fists up, the others clapping and waving, in their jetski kit (it stands on the bay); confetti and banners in the four seasons' colours, a low camera rising slowly to them, the champion's name (left of the gate's pylon, smaller or on two lines when long: `Podium.gateLeft`), and the final standings with points. A card in the whole year's colours covers the swap. Each screen builds it from what it already has (names, looks, points), so it needs nothing more from the race server. The ceremony is modelled (`art/scene/podium.py`, `render/podium.ts`): lacquered blocks with their numbers in the season's accent on a base striped in the four seasons; a gate close over the champion (two slim pylons, a header reading THE TILT, garlands), a banner of each season hanging outside it; a navy carpet on a paved apron; spectators each side behind sponsors' barriers who clap, cheer and take pictures; lamp posts, flowers and a palm; confetti and streamers on the GPU.

### Time trial

- **You alone, lapping a circuit, on our servers:** on the mode select, pick a circuit, a season it rides, the weather (the season's own; clear ranks, any other is practice: timed, not ranked, no ghost kept and none raced) and the time of day (only a look), and ride in a time trial room: one human, no bots, the weather held steady. There is no lap limit and **the time is the best lap**. On a loop every crossing of the start line ends a lap and starts the next (the first crossing, just off the grid, starts lap 1; the meter carries over). On a run a lap is the start signal to the finish; the rider rides out 4 s, then the sim takes them back to the top for a countdown, with no intro and no message. A lap with a missed buoy or gate is void. It is all sim state (`World::trial`, `Progress.laps`: the lap under way, laps ridden, the session's best and last; `LapTime` events), so a reload shows the right lap clock from a snapshot. Leaderboards are per circuit and season, best laps in clear weather. Time trials pay no credits and aren't matches (`match`, `race_result`): they have their own tables.
- **The session** runs until the player leaves (the race menu's Leave asks nothing: a session costs nothing to leave) or the room closes it: 3 minutes with the controls untouched (the input unchanged, so a phone left on auto-gas goes idle) or 60 minutes in all (`SessionOver`). Either way it ends on the paper panel a race ends on: the best lap, laps ridden, the ghosts raced (one you beat reads "Your previous best" or "Previous record"), your rank on the board, a new best or record called out, and why the room closed it if it did; Play again (a new session with the same picks), Change mode, Main menu.
- **Restart lap:** Y / △ / P or the race menu sends the lap under way back to the grid and straight to the countdown (the intro plays only once); the session's laps are kept. The ask rides in every input packet as a count, so it can't be lost.
- **The race server records every lap** (`crates/race-server/src/record.rs`, `protocol::ghost::LapRecorder`): the rider's authoritative state at snapshot rate (30 Hz) from the lap's start to its end, in the ghost format (`crates/protocol/src/ghost.rs`: a version byte, the circuit, its revision and the season, the lap's time, a split at every buoy, gate or checkpoint and the line, the rider's appearance bytes, then frames quantized to the network grid and predicted from the frames before; about 6 bytes a frame, 7 KB for a lap of the bay, 19 KB for a run). A lap goes to the lobby (`POST /api/servers/runs`, retried, with the shared secret) only if it is clean, in clear weather and the session's best so far; the rest are dropped.
- **The lobby keeps each player's best per circuit and season** with its ghost (`apps/lobby/src/ghosts.ts`; `time_trial_run` keeps every reported lap once, `time_trial_best` the best with its ghost under an id that never changes content). It takes laps only from a live race server with the secret, and only when the ghost's time, frames and splits match the report. The board ranks bests by time, ties to whoever set it first.
- **Circuit revisions:** every circuit has a revision (`Course::revision`; also in `@shifty/rider`'s circuits table and the conditions fixture), carried by ghosts, lap reports and the lobby's time trial rows. The lobby refuses laps of another revision, ranks only the current one, and lets the next clean lap replace a best of an older revision whatever its time, so old times stop ranking without anything being deleted. **Any change to a circuit's line, buoys, gates, obstacles, boats or ground bumps its revision.**
- **Ghosts:** by default you race your best and the record; the board's rows pick others (three at most). A ghost is that rider on their ride (`game/ghosts.ts`), played back by lap time between its frames, starting over each time the player begins a lap, through the same models and poses as live riders, in the ghost look (`render/cel/ghost.ts`): the rider's own paint washed toward a tint (magenta your best, gold the record, lime anyone else) and seen through, a rim of the tint on the silhouette and the outline at half strength. No collisions, wake, shadow or reflection, no name tag: a small see-through label over the head ("Your best", "Record", or the name). It fades as the camera comes close, and out once through its lap's end.
- **Splits:** in a time trial they come at every buoy, gate or checkpoint and the line, as your gap to the ghost quickest through that point of its lap (in practice weather, to your best lap of the session). As a lap ends its time is called out, with a new record, a new personal best or the session's best lap, or as void; a new best becomes your ghost a moment later and is kept through a reload. The HUD shows the lap's clock, the lap count, the best and the last lap, a void tag from the lap's first miss, and a practice tag when the weather isn't clear; no scoreboard or standings tower.

The login screen opens on the invite code (an invite link, `/invite/XXXX-XXXX`, fills it in and lands on "You're in!", where you make your account with GitHub or email); players who already have an account log in behind "Already invited? Log in". Making an account takes one use of the code. Nothing fails silently: an email or a GitHub without an account, and without an invite, is told so. Make links with `pnpm --filter lobby invite` (`--uses`, `--days`, `--note`, `--count`, `--list`; `--prod` for production's database and site).

A player's first single player is one race on the bay, the Tilt a pick away; the mode select remembers the last mode and each panel's picks through a race and a reload (`menu/picks.ts`). Kits and setups are picked by sport.

Focus is the accent filling the row, text turned to paper. Laid out on a 1920×1080 canvas (a window narrower than 16:9 gets one down to 1800 wide) and scaled to the window (`menu/scale.ts`, `--menu-zoom`; the race HUD has its own, `--ui-zoom`). Lists that scroll fade on the side where more is hidden, dialogs scroll their text with their buttons in view, and forms speak the game's own errors, never the browser's. Anything loading shows how far along it is (`loading.ts`): the boot screen and season cards with bars of their own, saying what loads, shorter waits (joining a race, a screen or art that takes a moment) with the loading sign (bottom right; bottom left on the phone canvas), with a slim bar when the load is counted. The "23.4° / SHIFTY" wordmark (the slash tilted 23.4°) is a placeholder logo. No component library (shadcn & co. look like a dashboard): our own handful of components.

### Customization

- **Your rider from presets, no sliders** (Mii Maker, Tony Hawk's create-a-skater): male or female, then a row of presets per part: skin (6), hair (5 per body), hair colour (6), eyes, eye colour, eyebrows, nose, mouth (5 each), beard and moustache (5, male only), height and build (5 each). Every body and face preset is free from the start, and you can change them any time. New players make their rider right after picking their handle.
- **Looks never touch the physics:** every body rides with the same hitbox and the same speed.
- **Clothes and gear per sport**, with made-up Costa Nevada brands named after the Mediterranean winds (Tramuntana, Garbí, Llevant, Xaloc, Ponent). Jetski (`summer` in the catalog): head, eyes, top, life vest, bottom, feet, jetski. Snowboard (`winter`): head, eyes, top, bottom, hands, feet, board. Each item is a model in one colourway (main, second and accent colours). Everyone starts with a free starter kit that already gives some variety, and **gear is cosmetic only**. Your jetski and snowboard kits swap with the sport in a Tilt. In cold conditions on the water (see Conditions) every rider wears cold-water gear under their jetski kit, with nothing to pick or buy: a black wetsuit from the neck to the wrists and ankles and gloves, its forearms and shins in the life vest's accent colour. It is the body's skin painted as neoprene along its regions (`WETSUIT` in `render/rider/model.ts`), so it needs no model.
- **Setups:** every rider spreads the same 12 points over speed, handling, stability and tricks (0-6 each), per sport. A point is a small change (top speed about 0.6%, turns and grip 4%, landing tolerance 8%, rotation speed 6%), so a setup is a style, not an advantage, and the budget is never earned. Set before entering a match; a match keeps the setups it started with (no changes between seasons). `cargo run -p sim --example setup_lab --release` races bots with each setup to keep them fair (racing builds within about ±0.6% of balanced).
- **The catalog, looks, setups and appearance bytes live in `packages/rider`** (`@shifty/rider`, shared by the client and the lobby), with the circuits and conditions tables (see Conditions). Item numbers are stable forever: add items at the end, never reuse a number.
- Riders are modelled in Blender (`art/rider/`; the head comes from MakeHuman's CC0 assets) with painted and baked textures and heroic proportions, exported to `apps/client/public/riders/` and drawn in the cel look. The same riders ride in races (`render/rider.ts` on the jetski, `render/boarder.ts` on the board, poses in `render/rider/ride.ts`). Both bodies are modelled, each with its five hair styles and both starter kits; an item whose model isn't built yet shows the starter's model in its own colours where the slot is required, and nothing elsewhere. The bodies share one builder (`art/rider/figure.py`; their tables in `male.py` and `female.py`), both 1.78 m tall, scaled to the height presets in the game; hair styles are in `art/rider/hairdo.py`. Eye, nose and mouth presets are MakeHuman shape targets (morph targets on the face), each with its own paint over the face (ink lines, the iris, and how its shape moves the shade line); brows and beards are painted overlays in the hair's colour. A body's anatomy is modelled in its silhouette (the shoulders' slope, the deltoid, the arm's and the leg's muscles) and drawn on its skin (`art/rider/lines.py`: strokes along each limb and round the trunk, on the rest pose): ink lines (collarbones, the deltoid's edge, elbows, knees, shins, the spine) and shapes the shade line is pushed into (under a pec, behind a knee), less of both on the female body. A garment's colours meet on hard edges (each texel of its mask is one colour).
- Rides are modelled in Blender too (`art/ride/build.py`): three jetskis (Garbí's runabout, Xaloc's racer, Ponent's cruiser: hull and deck lofted through their stations, footwells, seat, bars, bumper) and three boards (Tramuntana's freestyle twin, Llevant's freeride swallowtail, Xaloc's carver: outline, sidecut and kicks, with bindings), each painted with its brand's livery and wordmark, exported to `apps/client/public/rides/` and drawn in the cel look (`render/ride/`). A model is one mesh and one shader for all its colourways: the mask texture says where the main, second and accent colours go, and its alpha how glossy the paint is; the jetski's bars turn on their own bone. Every jetski puts the feet and hands in the same place and every board its bindings (the boots centred across it), so one pose fits all. A ride whose model isn't built yet shows the sport's starter ride in the item's colours. Bots wear starter clothes on any of the rides. The collection and the pro shop's rides show your ride with your rider (a board on its tail at their side, a jetski parked behind).

### Progression

Investigate what the greats did, copy where it makes sense, modern QoL where its better.

- **Credits** (SSX 3 / Tony Hawk 2 style) buy clothes and gear in the **pro shop at the country club**. Pointing at an item tries it on your rider. The collection tab is your wardrobe.
- Online races pay by finishing place (100, 85, 72, 62, 54, 47, 41, 36), plus style (trick points ÷ 25, at most 80 a race) and 80 for finishing a Tilt. Matchmaking always pays, even when bots fill the seats. A private room pays only races with two or more people in it. Single player never pays.
- Pacing: an average Tilt earns about a fifth of an average item. Missions and unlocks you can't buy come later.
- The lobby keeps a **ledger**: every change to a player's credits with its reason, and each race pays out once (retried reports pay nothing more). Buying checks ownership and balance in one transaction. The end of a race shows what the ledger paid for that match (`me/matches/:id`), never a sum the client made up.

### Architecture

```
Browser (Vite + Three.js + TypeScript, Rust sim compiled to WASM)
 ├─ HTTPS / WebSocket ──► Lobby: TypeScript Worker + Durable Objects
 │                        site, login, matchmaking, rooms, Postgres
 │                             │ race servers register here
 │                             │ lobby signs a "join race" token
 │                             ▼
 └─ WebTransport ───────► Race server: native Rust (Docker)
      datagrams: inputs + snapshots (unreliable)
      stream:    start/finish/tricks/chat (reliable)
```

- **Race traffic is WebTransport (UDP) only**. We use the browser so players don't have to download anything, not to reach office/school networks that block UDP. Like console games: if your network blocks UDP, you can't race online. The lobby (menus, matchmaking) still uses HTTPS/WebSocket, which is fine since it isn't real-time racing.

### Netcode

- **Server-authoritative**, Rocket League / Quake model (not peer-to-peer FGC rollback).
- Clients send **inputs only**, numbered per tick. Never positions.
- Server runs the sim at a fixed tick rate and sends snapshots.
- **Client prediction + reconciliation** for your own rider (this is our "rollback where it makes sense").
- **Other riders are predicted too**: everyone is drawn in the predicted present, extrapolated with their last known input and smoothed on correction, so what you see is what you bump into. No interpolation mode for live riders.
- **Ghosts and replays are playback, not netcode:** the server's authoritative snapshot stream of a run (or a whole race) is the recording; the client plays it back by race time. Independent from the live prediction above.
- **Server-side scoring and timing.** Ghosts and leaderboards only come from server-recorded runs, never from client uploads.
- Inputs are validated (clamped, rate-limited, max lag).
- **Protocol designed for UDP**: self-contained or delta snapshots against an acknowledged baseline, redundant inputs, important events with IDs that are resent until acknowledged. Transport sits behind an interface (`send` / `onMessage`).
- Snapshots must fit one datagram (about 1,200 bytes; Chrome reports 1,211 for WebTransport). Use compact binary encoding.
- **Numbers:** sim at 60 Hz, snapshots at 30 Hz. **4 humans max per room**, 8 rider slots so bots can fill the field.
- **Determinism:** native (server) and WASM (browser) sims must be bit-identical. No SIMD math crates, no platform libm (use `sim::math`, backed by `libm`), no `mul_add`, no hash-map iteration in the sim. Authoritative state is snapped onto the network grid every tick, so snapshots are lossless and re-simulation does not drift. CI checks native vs WASM hashes (`pnpm check:determinism`).
- **Rider collisions: yes.** The server resolves them; clients predict them by running the whole world forward (other humans extrapolated by repeating their last input, bots predicted exactly because their AI is deterministic sim code). Riders bump as capsules along their heading (`crates/sim/src/capsule.rs`, `Sport::body`: a jetski's hull, 3.2 × 1.2 m; a board ridden along its length, 1.6 × 0.6 m), pushed apart where their closest points meet, never turned; land, the breakwater and moored boats stop the hull too. A rider right on the chase camera is left out of the picture (`CAMERA_CLEAR`).
- **Bots** are server-side riders whose inputs come from `sim::bot` instead of the network, through the same sim path. Single player builds on this. Each seat has a pace tier and is a trickster or not (`bot::JETSKI_SEATS` and `bot::SNOW_SEATS`, in the order bots fill seats: the fastest within a few percent of the best clean lap, the slowest beatable by a new player); a trickster takes only the ramps, tables and lips on its own line. Bots ride a line through their buoys or gates, steer round what stands in their way, and one held up against something goes round it on the side it is on: no bot is ever stuck (a test holds it over both runs, every season and two weathers).
- **Autopilot is sim state:** a rider through the finish (for the rest of the race), one who joined mid-race (until the next race) and everyone on the results ride with `sim::bot` inputs chosen inside `World::step` (`World::on_autopilot`), whatever their player sends. The server and every screen agree without a message, and such riders are predicted exactly on every screen, their own and others'. They only bump each other, never riders still racing.
- **Clock sync:** the server reports how early each client's inputs arrive; clients speed up or slow down up to 6% (hard resync when far off) to keep a 2-tick lead. A hard resync that takes the predicted clock back never takes the race back across the start of the phase on screen (a countdown that is over never runs again): it comes back that far, and the time scale drifts the rest.
- **A race starts when everyone has loaded:** every input packet says which race of the plan its screen has on show (course loaded, season card lifted). A race waits on the grid until everyone the lobby sent (the ticket's `humans`) has arrived and every connected screen is ready, or for 30 s at most (a late screen joins the race wherever it is). People who left don't count; bots never wait. The server then starts the intro (7 s), and the countdown (4 s) and the race follow by the sim's clock, so clients predict them; race phases are sim state, numbered for the wire (grid 0, countdown 1, racing 2, results 3, intro 4). `devkit bots --loading <s>` plays a screen that loads slowly.
- **Going quiet:** a screen still loading its race may go silent for as long as the grid waits (30 s) before the room drops it; once its race is on show, 10 s, the same as the connection's QUIC idle timeout (browsers answer keep-alives while a page is frozen, so QUIC only ends connections whose browser has gone). A player quiet for over a second lets go of the controls.
- **Coming back:** a connection that goes without a goodbye (a reload, a closed tab, the network) keeps its seat for 20 s (`AWAY_GRACE_MS`), the rider coasting on no input, and a new screen for the same player takes it back with the same rider (it gets the roster from the start). Leaving on purpose says goodbye (`Bye`), and the seat goes at once. Past the grace, a Tilt under way keeps the seat with a bot, riding for nobody, for its player: they take it back, with their points, riding free until the next race, whenever they return while the Tilt runs; a room holding seats waits three minutes with nobody in it. A rejoin ticket (`rejoin` in the claims) only seats a player in a room still running, and never starts one. The race a tab is in survives a reload (session storage: its ticket, where it goes back to, the plan) and comes back to the same seat; the mode select offers a matchmaking race still under way back (from `me/live`), unless it was left on purpose. Single player and time trials come back only through a reload.
- **A plan's end:** the last race of a room's plan holds its results (`World::hold_results`; a time trial's too), set alike by the race server and by every client from where the race stands in the plan (`event_index`, `event_count`), so everyone rides on on autopilot, predicted exactly, behind what comes after the race. Once the results have been up `RESULTS_TICKS` the plan is over (`Room::is_over`): the room refuses everyone (and for 15 minutes tokens signed before the end), leaves the server's room count and the heartbeat's humans (`watching` in `/metrics`), and lives on while anyone is still in it, five minutes at most (`AFTER_PLAN_TICKS`; `Room::is_done`). A rider who leaves then rides on as a bot, unannounced. Draining doesn't wait for it, and a screen whose room closes under it says nothing. Screens read the plan their ticket races (`planOf` in sim-wasm, `JoinClaims::room_plan`: the same draw the race server makes) to say what comes next and to put the right season card up; what is raced still comes from snapshots.
- **Netcode lives in `crates/client-core`** (pure Rust: prediction, reconciliation, clock sync, smoothing). The browser uses it through `sim-wasm`; headless bots and tests use it natively. The race server's `Room` is a pure state machine too, so `crates/race-server/tests/netcode.rs` tests the whole loop over a simulated lossy network.

### Race server

- One Rust binary, many rooms (one Tokio task per room). Docker image based on distroless, about 10-20 MB.
- WebTransport with **`web-transport-quinn` + `quinn`** (the stack behind Cloudflare's production MoQ relay).
- Ports: 4433/UDP (WebTransport, all game traffic). 4433/TCP serves only ops HTTP (`/healthz`, `/metrics`, `/cert-hash`), never published in production. Docker needs `-p 4433:4433/udp` explicitly, or `--network host`. The image's `HEALTHCHECK` runs `race-server healthcheck` (GETs `/healthz` inside the container; 503 while draining).
- Config through env vars: `LOBBY_URL`, `RACE_SERVER_SECRET`, `REGION`, `PUBLIC_ADDR`, `LOBBY_PUBLIC_KEY`, plus `BIND` (default `[::]:4433`), `TLS_CERT`/`TLS_KEY` (domain mode), `DRAIN_TIMEOUT_SECS`, `RUST_LOG`.
- Join tokens: `base64url(claims JSON) "." base64url(Ed25519 sig over "shifty-join-v1." + payload)`. Claims carry `aud` = the race server's `PUBLIC_ADDR`, so a token only works on the server it was issued for, the room's plan (`tilt`, or `races`: one spec each, fields left out are drawn) and the `seed` it is drawn with (the same in every ticket for a room, rejoins included), `humans` (how many people the lobby sent, so the first race waits for them), `look` (the rider's appearance bytes, passed on to the room unread) and `setups` (per sport, checked again by the sim), and `trial` (a time trial: one race with its circuit and a season it rides picked). The lobby checks a rider only wears what they own before signing. Every `RiderJoined` event tells the room each rider's look and setups (clients need the setups to predict them), and bots dress alike on every screen. Format lives in `crates/protocol/src/token.rs`; the lobby signs them (`apps/lobby/src/tickets.ts`; a test pins one token both sides must agree on). `scripts/dev-lobby.ts` signs dev quick-race tickets with the same dev key.
- Paths: WebTransport `/race`; ops HTTP `/healthz`, `/metrics`, `/cert-hash`.
- On boot it registers with the lobby and sends heartbeats (region, load).
- Verifies join tokens with the lobby's **public key only**. A race server can check tokens but never create them.
- Heartbeats to the lobby every 5 s (address, region, version, certificate hashes, rooms, humans, draining) and reports every finished race (`POST /api/servers/results`, retried: its course and conditions, its place in the plan and the plan's length, and every rider's result), both with the shared `RACE_SERVER_SECRET`. **Race servers never touch Postgres.**
- Versions must match: the lobby only sends players to race servers of its own version (the Cargo workspace version, one for the whole game), tickets carry it, and a client on another build asks the player to reload instead of racing.
- A room's plan is over once its last results have had their time; the room then closes when nobody is left in it, or five minutes later (a time trial's at its 15-minute cap), and for 15 minutes refuses tickets issued before the end (`iat` in the claims).
- Graceful shutdown on SIGTERM: no new rooms, wait for the current plans to end (`DRAIN_TIMEOUT_SECS`, 900 by default), then exit.
- `/healthz`, logs to stdout, tick-time metrics.
- TLS, two modes:
  - **Domain mode:** real certificate (Let's Encrypt) mounted into the container.
  - **Hash mode:** short-lived self-signed cert generated at boot, hash sent to the lobby, browsers trust it via `serverCertificateHashes`. No domain needed (LAN, friend's PC). ECDSA P-256, valid 13 days (Chrome rejects more than 14), rotated every 6 days without dropping sessions. The next certificate is made an hour before the switch: heartbeats and tickets carry every live hash (the current one first), so tickets signed around the switch still connect.

### Lobby

- `apps/lobby`: a Worker (Hono) with **Better Auth** (GitHub OAuth and email one-time codes, sessions in Postgres, the admin plugin for roles and bans) on **Drizzle + Postgres** (`src/db/schema.ts`; migrations in `drizzle/`, made with `pnpm --filter lobby db:generate`). Postgres comes from Hyperdrive in production, `DATABASE_URL` in dev and self-hosting.
- Durable Objects: `RoomLobby` (one per room code: members over hibernatable WebSockets, host settings (the plan request and the bots), ready, start; the race it started keeps its plan and seed for rejoin tickets), `Matchmaker` (the Tilt queue: a race starts with four, or when the first in line has waited `MATCH_WAIT_SECS`, 20 by default; bots fill the seats), `ServerRegistry` (race servers from their heartbeats; picks the least busy one on the lobby's version).
- API under `/api`: `health` (the lobby's version and the live race servers' versions, no addresses), `auth/*` (Better Auth), `me` (with the rider: appearance, setups, credits, owned items), `me/handle`, `me/card`, `me/rider`, `me/setups`, `me/earnings`, `me/matches/:id` (what a match you raced paid you, race by race, from the ledger, which of its races the lobby has results for, your best place on that circuit before it, and your credits now), `me/live` (the matchmaking race you are still in, started in the last half hour with its race server up, with a rejoin ticket), `shop/buy`, `content`, `play/single` (the Tilt, or one race with its picks), `play/time-trial` (a circuit, a season, the weather and the time of day), `time-trial/board` (a circuit and season: the best ten, your row and rank), `time-trial/ghosts/:id`, `rooms`, `rooms/:code/ws`, `matchmaking/ws`; race servers use `servers/heartbeat`, `servers/results` (which also pays the race's credits) and `servers/runs` (time trial runs). Our own endpoints only take requests from the game's origin.
- Secrets: `BETTER_AUTH_SECRET`, `LOBBY_SIGNING_KEY` (Ed25519 seed for join tokens), `RACE_SERVER_SECRET`, `GITHUB_CLIENT_ID` / `GITHUB_CLIENT_SECRET`; `BETTER_AUTH_URL` is the public origin. Email: `MAIL_MODE=console` prints codes (dev); production sends them through Cloudflare Email Service (the `EMAIL` binding, from `MAIL_FROM` = `noreply@4shifty.gg`; `4shifty.gg` is onboarded for sending). workerd has no Email Service, so self-hosted lobbies log the codes as errors for now. `DEV_MAILBOX=1` (dev and tests only) exposes the last code per address at `/api/dev/mailbox`. `SIGNUPS=open` lets anyone make an account; otherwise new accounts need an invite code (`src/invites.ts`, the `invite` table).
- Client and lobby share one origin (the lobby serves `/api`), so the session cookie just works.
- TypeScript Worker + Durable Objects. Runs on Cloudflare, or self-hosted with **`workerd`** (Cloudflare's open-source runtime) in Docker. Same code.
- **Durable Object state is temporary only** (rooms, presence, matchmaking queues), all rebuildable. **Postgres is the source of truth** (accounts, results, ghosts). This also makes workerd's experimental on-disk storage low-risk for self-hosters.
- Use WebSocket hibernation when idle so idle rooms cost nothing.

### Engine

Three.js, using **`WebGPURenderer` + TSL** (Three.js Shading Language) from `three/webgpu` and `three/tsl`, never the classic `WebGLRenderer` + hand-written GLSL. Browsers without WebGPU fall back to WebGL2 automatically. The three.js version is pinned (TSL still changes between releases); upgrade on purpose.

- **Water:** physics-relevant swells are a pure function in `crates/sim` (sum of directional waves); the client shader evaluates the same parameters, so the water you see is the water the physics rides. Visual-only detail (FFT chop, foam, spray, wake textures) lives in the client and never touches physics. No paid water kits: their licenses forbid a public repo.
- **Nothing compiles a shader mid-frame.** Shader compiles block the page (Safari's WebGPU, every WebGL2), so a stage's shaders compile ahead with three's `compileAsync`, which doesn't (`Engine.warm`; riders whose art arrives later warm up through `render/warm.ts`). Nothing is drawn meanwhile: the canvas holds its last frame under the boot screen, the loading sign or the season card (a race's course loads behind its card while the netcode keeps running), and the boot screen and the card stay up until what the screen shows is in (its riders, `Stage.loaded`) and frames run without a stall (`Engine.settled`). The page's entry (`main.tsx`) is a small script that puts the loading sign's "Loading…" up in the player's language; the game (`start.tsx`) loads after it. The club's terrace (mode select) loads behind the main menu (`Engine.preload`), so Play shows it at once. Environments keep one `Fog` each: a new one rebuilds every shader drawn in it. What only some weather shows (rain, snowfall, gulls) is flagged `userData.weather` and compiled shown, so it never compiles when the weather brings it. A rider's colours and size repaint in place; only a new shape or outfit rebuilds them. A material's numbers are uniforms, not constants, so materials that differ only in them share one shader. Rider textures decode off the main thread (`ImageBitmapLoader`). Check stalls in Safari's engine too (Playwright `webkit`), not only Chromium.
- **Loading shows how far along it is** (`loading.ts`): downloads count bytes (`download`; art only through `render/art.ts`), warm-ups count shaders (`Engine.warm`), the rest says when it starts and ends (`Wait.begin`, `tracked`). A wait (the boot screen, a season card, or the loading sign) weighs its steps by how long they took in this browser last time (`shifty.loads`), never goes back, and fills as its cover lifts; background work goes `quietly` or with a null wait. Dev: `window.__loads()`. **Art is asked for by content hash** (`virtual:art`, `artUrl`), so `public/_headers` keeps it, and the built files, for good. **Courses build in slices** (`render/slices.ts`: generators run by `buildSliced` in races and the menus, `buildNow` elsewhere; a piece stays well under 50 ms; `Stage.build` holds drawing meanwhile), so the netcode keeps its cadence under the card. A race's first frames behind its card draw every caster into the shadow map, spread over six frames (`World.warmShadows`); a card opened under the boot screen (a reload into a race) takes the course's load. The race screen is its own chunk, fetched after boot (`afterBoot`); the club's art downloads at startup, a picked run's models on the mode select, and a Tilt fetches the other sport's art during results.
- **60 frames a second on modest machines.** Resolution is automatic unless the player fixes it in Settings (`game/resolution.ts`; a touch screen starts at two device pixels per CSS pixel: a phone's three would be millions of pixels for a small GPU; a window resize keeps the scale found): fewer pixels when frames come slower than 60 a second, more again once they keep up, and a step that doesn't help (short of CPU, a slow display) is undone. Pixels stay cheap: no Perlin noise per pixel where the noise texture (`render/cel/noise.ts`) or the vertex stage will do, what never changes is baked (the bay's shore distance), and the sky draws last so only uncovered pixels shade it. Draws stay few: static scenery merges per material (`render/merge.ts`), things that repeat or float are instanced (a thing that repeats under a dozen times is merged instead: `Planting.build(true)`), what stands far from the water (far scenery and cumulus too) stays out of the mirror (`UNREFLECTED`) and the shadow map, shade under trees, parasols and people beyond the sun's shadow box is a flat shape in the ground's own shade tone (`coast/grounding.ts`: one tone with cast shadows, gone under a closed sky), walkers are one mesh moved in the vertex shader, models made of boxes are one vertex-coloured mesh (`render/parts.ts`), and identical cel materials are made once. Riders are drawn only in view, outlined within 45 m, reflected (the body alone) within 30 m and cast shadows inside the sun's shadow box. Measure with `?bench=1`: `window.__shiftyBench` draws the held frame back to back (`bench(n)`: CPU and total ms a frame) with parts turned off (`ablate("noink,noshadow")`; `meshes()` and `broken()` list the scene's meshes and those with positions that aren't numbers), in WebKit too.
- **Audio:** every sound is ours, synthesized with Web Audio (oscillators, filtered noise, envelopes): no samples, no voices. One-shots (the countdown, chimes, splashes, landings, crashes, menu ticks) are recipes rendered once at boot through an `OfflineAudioContext`, the menus' first (`audio/library.ts`). The ride's continuous sounds (the jetski's engine, a four-stroke triple's growl through fixed hull resonances with a loping idle and the pump's rush, and its hull; the board on the snow; the wind) are live voices driven every frame by the predicted riders (`audio/voices.ts`, `audio/race.ts`): yours in full, the three nearest other riders as cheap voices placed from the camera (distance, pan, air absorption, Doppler). The countdown beeps follow the sim's countdown phase, tick by tick, and the wrong-way cue the HUD's steadied warning. One `AudioContext`, made by the first key or click (browsers hold sound until then; a gamepad press doesn't count, so while sound waits and a gamepad is in use a small sign in the bottom margin says to click or press a key, `audio/hint.ts`) and suspended in hidden tabs: an sfx bus and a music bus into a master and a limiter (`audio/index.ts`). Music isn't in the repo: a player fetches tracks at runtime and plugs into the music bus (`audio.onAudioReady`), which ducks under the countdown and the results; with no tracks the game just has no music. Master, effects and music volumes are in Settings, saved in the browser. The menus tick through `menu/nav.ts`.

### Graphics

**The look is cel-shaded**, like an anime or a hand drawing: Guilty Gear Strive and Dragon Ball Z are the bar, XIII the floor. Code in `apps/client/src/render/cel/`.

- Every surface has two tones, lit and shade, split on a hard line. Shade is a cool tone, richer than the colour. Cast shadows fall in the shade tone, and the shade side catches a rim of sky light.
- Ink lines come from a post pass: silhouettes from depth, creases from normals, edges between objects. Each line is a darker version of the colour under it.
- Riders get an outline (an inverted hull). Their normals bend toward simple shapes (an ellipsoid head whose face is flattened into one plane, capsule limbs) so shade falls in clean shapes: a face is lit whole until the light is well round its side, the neck sits in the head's shade. The body's skin is one flat colour carrying its drawn anatomy (`body.skin.lines.png`): ink lines cut to a steady width on screen at any distance, like the outline, and pushes of the shade line. Painted lines (lashes, brows, irises) and the edges between a mask's colours are cut crisp in the shader at any zoom, and the shapes of shade a face always carries (under the nose, the lower lip and the upper lid) are painted as pushes of the shade line. The head and the hands are drawn a little larger than the art (`render/rider/rig.ts`). Stunts and tricks are held on twos; standing poses move smoothly (a slow idle on twos reads as flicker). A face takes no cast shadows (hair and hats would throw blocks of shade across it), and under a coarse shadow map (a race's covers 80 m) riders look it up from half a metre toward the sun (`SHADOW_LIFT`), so they are shaded by what stands between them and the sun, never by blocks of their own limbs.
- Standing riders (`render/rider/poses.ts`) stand at ease in contrapposto, the weight on one leg and shifting to the other now and then, breathing, blinking and looking about; with a hand on the hip on the main menu, and arms crossed on the mode select. A pose can say what the face does (`Pose.face`): a blink is the upper lid coming down over the eye in the shader, from the face's eyelid map (`body.face.lids.png`), to one line along the lower lid; the eyes glance a little ahead of the head's turn, their irises sliding inside the opening.
- The sky is a painted gradient with cel cumulus. The sea is banded (shelves near the shore, strokes along the swells, cut-out foam) and still rides the sim's swells.

Blender for models. Characters are low-poly; environments, VFX, shadows and lighting aim for PS2/GC fidelity. Modern techniques are welcome, and so is rendering at a lower resolution than we could. Detailed environments with low-poly characters are fine. Every look change is checked side by side against references.

Blender runs headless for asset work: `/Applications/Blender.app/Contents/MacOS/Blender --background --python-expr ...` (glTF export works).

The mountain's scenery models are built by `art/env/build.py` (Python that writes each mesh corner by corner: `trees.py`, `rocks.py`, `buildings.py`, `props.py`) into `apps/client/public/env/<place>.glb`: one mesh per model, vertex colours for its tones, and two masks in its UV that the place's shaders read (on trees: how far along a bough, how readily snow lies; on rocks and roofs: a snow cap's height and whether it is one). `render/env/models.ts` loads them once; an environment that needs them exposes `ready`, which the race waits on before compiling. `/Applications/Blender.app/Contents/MacOS/Blender --background --factory-startup --python art/env/build.py` rebuilds them.

The club's and the podium's scenery (`art/scene/`, built by `art/scene/build.py -- [--only club,podium]` under Blender into `public/env/<scene>.glb`) is triangle soups with a colour per vertex (alpha: how far the wind sways it), no normals or textures, 16-bit positions, one material class per mesh (`solid`, `foliage`, `cloth`, `glass`, `paving`), props placed many times, `anim` groups the client moves and anchors (`sign`, `wind`). `render/env/model.ts` joins each class into one geometry a few milliseconds at a time; `render/env/materials.ts` has the cel material of each class; `render/env/signs.ts` letters `sign` anchors at runtime in Archivo. Simple cel people come from `art/scene/people.py`. Each part of a scene has a level of detail (`extras.lod`: always, from close by, from afar; `Scene.level`, `EnvModel.near` / `far`). Art shared between environments is marked `userData.shared`, and `disposeTree` leaves it. `EnvLook(false)` makes materials that skip the weather (the podium). This scenery is left out of the sea's mirror image.

**Coast scenery is built in code** (`render/coast/`): `shape.ts` gathers painted triangles into one mesh per material; `rock.ts` is the cel rock (strata in beds, a wet band under a salt line) with cliffs swept along shores traced from the sim's capsules (`shoreline.ts`), stacks, crags and armour stone; `town.ts` lays a town on a quay capsule from a sheet of facade tiles painted at boot (`facades.ts`); `harbour.ts` (breakwater, mole, lighthouse, lights that flash), `beach.ts`, `landmarks.ts`, `trees.ts` (stone pines, palms, cypresses, agaves; `Planting` batches or merges them), `boats.ts` (`Fleet`: every moored boat in one mesh, riding the swell in the vertex shader; `Roamer`: a boat under way), `people.ts` (figures; `Crowd` waves at riders; `crowdOf(conditions)`: full in summer, a quarter in winter, almost nobody in a storm), `life.ts` (flags). Foliage and cloth move with `LOOK.wind`. No model files: nothing to load.

**The distance** (`render/scenery.ts`): a plain, `foothills` with terraces and white villages, the `sierra` (ranges of `peakGeometry` merged into one mesh) and `seaHorizon` (far headlands, islets, a ferry); each fades by hand (`distanceHaze`), never by the scene's fog. The sky has the sun's disc, `LOOK.puffs` (soft cloud bands on fair days) and `LOOK.veil` (rain under the deck); a clear sky keeps large calm areas, and dense streaks are for windy weather.

## Workflow

- **Run everything:** `pnpm install`, then `pnpm dev` (Postgres in a container, the lobby, the race server, Vite), then open `http://localhost:5173` and log in: sign-in codes show up in the lobby's log. For GitHub sign-in put a dev OAuth app (callback `http://localhost:5173/api/auth/callback/github`) in `.dev/lobby.env` as `GITHUB_CLIENT_ID=` / `GITHUB_CLIENT_SECRET=`. Postgres keeps running after `pnpm dev` (`container stop shifty-postgres`); set `DATABASE_URL` to use another one. A second checkout runs beside the first with its own ports and database (`VITE_PORT`, `LOBBY_PORT`, `RACE_PORT`, `DATABASE_URL`), or only its own Vite against the first's lobby (`LOBBY_ORIGIN=http://localhost:5173`, `vite --port`). **Quick race without the menus**: `http://localhost:5173/?name=You` in two windows. URL options (all dev-only, see below): `event` (`snow` = the mountain run, `gp` = the Tilt, or a circuit's id or name, as `cape` or `glades`; the bay otherwise), `season` (a name or its number; summer on the water and winter on the snow by default), `weather` and `turns` (names or numbers), `time` (10-minute steps from midnight, `HH:MM`, or `dawn`/`morning`/`noon`/`afternoon`/`dusk`), `seed` (the draw of whatever is left out, 0 by default; the room's name follows these options, since a room keeps the plan it was made with), `room`, `bots` (0-7), `surface` (0 flat, 1 swells as designed, 2 calm, 3 rough), `scale` (render scale), `water` (`basic` = swells only, for slow machines; the default adds GPU chop, ripples, wake and shore foam, and reflections), `render=0` (nothing drawn: netcode and menu tests; works on the menus too), `lookdev=1` (dev: riders on the club terrace for look testing; `&ride=1` puts them on the sport's ride, with `steer`, `lean`, `grab`, `air`, `bail`, and on a jetski `stunt` (1-5) and `reach` (0-1)), `celshot=flyover|club|hero|menu` (dev: cel look test shots; `menu` is a rider on the main menu's colours, `shot=hero|body|face`, with the look test's rider options; `inkdebug=line|normal|ink|depth` shows the ink pass), `celshot=course` (dev: a circuit in its conditions without a race, with the quick race's `event`, `season`, `weather`, `turns` and `time`, `turn` (0-1, how far the weather has turned), and `view` (a rider along the course, or wide), `at=<m down the run>` (a rider there; `off` metres off the centre line, `back=<m>` seen from below looking back up) or `eye=x,y,z&look=x,y,z`, `fly=0-1` (the intro's fly-over at that moment), and on a run `tv=<camera>&rider=<m down the run>&speed=<m/s>` (a television camera on a rider there)), `autopilot=1` (dev: ride with the bot's inputs, to look around a course; in races started from the menus too; `autopilot=miss` passes every buoy on its wrong side and rides outside every gate), `podium=1` (dev: the end-of-Tilt podium with sample riders; `season`, `riders`, `me`, `seed`), `bench=1` (dev: the frame-cost bench, see Engine), `sfx` (dev: the sound board: plays every sound, with sliders for the engine, the board and a rider passing by). F1 toggles the debug panel (stats, fake lag/jitter/loss). `SIGNUPS=invite pnpm dev` tries the invite gate (dev is open otherwise).
- **URL switches, test hooks and debug tools are dev-server only.** A production build reads nothing from the URL's query (the router reads only its own routes, like `/play?mode=`). Players set resolution, water and volumes in Settings. Every switch is read through `apps/client/src/dev-params.ts`, behind `import.meta.env.DEV`. Lint forbids reading `location.search` / `searchParams` anywhere else in the client, and `pnpm build` fails if the bundle has a switch read outside `import.meta.env.DEV`, the quick race (`/__dev/`), the e2e hooks (`window.__shifty`), the debug panel or the sound board.
- **Physics tuning:** `cargo run -p sim --example jetski_lab --release [seed]` prints the jetski's draft, top speed, how turns build up and carry on, how long each rotation, stunt and combo takes off a ramp (and the least air each stunt needs), and how often and how high swells launch it and how deep it dives (into, with and across the swell, with and without pulling back). `jetski_lab [seed] [surface] [course]` ends with the sea on a water circuit by conditions (wave heights and steepness at the start and the end, the wind, the air off the crests), and on the cape how much swell reaches each part of it (the harbour, the arch, the beach, the point, the stacks). `cargo run -p sim --example bot_race --release [seed] [course] [season weather [turns] [time]]` runs a bots-only race on any circuit in any conditions and prints times, missed buoys, tricks, points and bails, and which tricks were landed (`BOT_TRACE=<slot>` follows one bot); `bot_race table [seed]` races every circuit in every season its sport rides, in a spread of weathers, and prints the times against its home conditions', the falls, the tricks, the misses and the disqualifications; `bot_race kinds [seeds] [course…]` prints wins, places and times by bot kind and seat; `bot_race best` rides a jetski bot alone flat out with a full meter, then each tier. `cargo run -p sim --example snow_lab --release [mountain|glades] [seed]` (`snow_lab kinds`: wins by bot kind against the best a bot does alone) prints each run's profile (pitch, banks, widths), snowboard speeds standing, tucked and braking, the air off every table, lip and drop with and without a pop, a bots-only race down the run (times, falls and where, tricks, points, cost per tick), and the snow by season and weather (top, middle and bottom, a tuck's speed, the bots' times against the home conditions') with fall's rocks. Circuits go by id or name (`oval`, `mountain`...). Conditions keep the bots within about ±10% of their home time (a winter storm on the water is the slowest).
- **Checks:** `cargo test --workspace`, `cargo clippy --workspace --all-targets -- -D warnings`, `cargo deny check`, `pnpm audit`, `pnpm check:determinism`, `pnpm lint`, `pnpm fmt:check`, `pnpm typecheck`, `pnpm --filter lobby test`, `pnpm --filter @shifty/rider test`, `pnpm test:e2e` (menus against the real lobby, and the netcode). CI runs all of them on every PR. The race server image (`.github/workflows/image.yml`) builds on every push to `main`, and on PRs only when packaging changes (Dockerfile, Cargo manifests and lockfile, toolchain, notices config). CI actions are pinned by commit SHA (with the version in a comment).
- **Tooling:** pnpm, TypeScript 7 (the Go-native `tsc`), Vite 8, **oxlint** (type-aware) and **oxfmt** for TS/JSON/MD/CSS. Their configs changed a lot recently: read the current docs before changing `.oxlintrc.json` / `.oxfmtrc.json`. Rust: rustfmt + clippy, toolchain pinned in `rust-toolchain.toml`.
- **WASM:** `pnpm build:wasm` (cargo + `wasm-bindgen` CLI, which must match the `wasm-bindgen` version in `Cargo.lock`). Generated files in `apps/client/src/wasm/` are not committed.
- **Deploy:** the game lives at `https://23.4shifty.gg` (`4shifty.gg` and `www` redirect there). Merging the release-please PR publishes the release, and `.github/workflows/deploy.yml` deploys it: the race server image to GHCR (`ghcr.io/karjona/shifty-race-server:X.Y.Z`), Neon migrations, the Worker (which serves `apps/client/dist`; `wrangler versions upload` then `versions deploy`, so the token never touches the custom domain: change routes or domains by hand with `wrangler triggers deploy`), then the race server through Coolify (moves `:live` to the version, redeploys, and waits until the lobby sees it; the old server finishes its races first). To redeploy or roll back, run Deploy on a release tag. Secrets live in the GitHub `production` environment (tags `v*` only): `CLOUDFLARE_API_TOKEN` (account token, Workers Editor on `shifty-lobby` only), `NEON_DATABASE_URL`, `COOLIFY_TOKEN` (deploy-only); variables `CLOUDFLARE_ACCOUNT_ID`, `COOLIFY_URL`, `COOLIFY_APP_UUID`. Worker secrets and the race server's env vars are set once by hand (`wrangler secret put`, the Coolify app); their values live in `.dev/prod.env` and `.dev/prod-race-server.env` (gitignored). By hand, if ever needed: `pnpm build`, then `pnpm --filter lobby exec wrangler deploy --secrets-file ../../.dev/prod.env`, and `pnpm --filter lobby db:migrate` with `.dev/neon.env` loaded.
- **Migrations must keep working with the previous release's code** (add first, drop a release later): the old Worker keeps serving while a deploy runs.
- **Containers:** Kilian's Mac has no Docker; use Apple's `container` CLI (Docker-compatible): `container build -f crates/race-server/Dockerfile -t shifty-race-server .`. For UDP from the host use `127.0.0.1`, not `::1`.
- **Git:** never push to `main`. Work on a branch per piece of work and open a PR; PRs are squash-merged, so the PR title becomes the commit on `main`.
  - **PR titles are Conventional Commits** (`feat: …`, `fix(netcode): …`), checked in CI by commitlint. Scopes (optional): `sim`, `protocol`, `netcode`, `server`, `client`, `lobby`, `devkit`, `art`, `audio`, `ci`, `deps`. Local commits are checked too, by a lefthook `commit-msg` hook that `pnpm install` sets up.
  - **PR bodies are one short paragraph at most** (what and why). Details go in commits, AGENTS.md or code.
- **Releases:** release-please keeps a release PR open on `main`; merging it tags `vX.Y.Z` and publishes a GitHub release with the release notes (there is no `CHANGELOG.md`; GitHub releases are the changelog). **One version for the whole game** (Cargo workspace, `Cargo.lock`, every `package.json`), 0.x for now: `feat` and breaking changes bump the minor, `fix` and `perf` the patch. It runs as a GitHub App (`RELEASE_APP_CLIENT_ID` variable, `RELEASE_APP_PRIVATE_KEY` secret) so CI runs on its PRs.
- **Dependencies:** Dependabot opens weekly grouped PRs (actions, cargo, npm, Rust toolchain, Docker base images; `three` comes on its own). `cargo deny check` (`deny.toml`) allows only permissive licenses, known sources and no RustSec advisories; `pnpm audit` checks npm packages for advisories (GitHub's dependency graph can't read pnpm's two-document lockfile, so Dependabot alerts miss most npm packages). Shipped builds carry third-party notices rendered by `cargo-about` (`about.toml`, `about.hbs`): the race server image in `/usr/share/doc/shifty/`, the client in `dist/licenses/` (`rust.md` for the WASM sim, `npm.md` from Vite), so `pnpm build` needs `cargo-about` installed.
